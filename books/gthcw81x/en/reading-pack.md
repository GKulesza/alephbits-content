## Text

**GRANDMA RÓZIA'S KEY**

Lena was ten years old and had hair the colour of honey, which always smelled of hay and wild flowers. She lived at the edge of a village, where days began with the crowing of a rooster and ended with the smell of freshly baked bread. Her parents ran a farm, and Lena loved every part of it — from the hens that greeted her with clucking every morning, to the cow MUCZA, who gave the tastiest milk in the world.

One morning, as she was collecting eggs into a basket, she heard her parents talking through the half-open window. Dad's voice was heavy, as if it carried the whole weight of the sky.

– If it doesn't rain soon, the crops will die. There'll be no supplies for the winter.

The words pierced Lena's heart like a thorn. She walked around pensive for the rest of the day, and in the evening, when the sun hid behind the hills, she ran to grandma Rózia's little house.

Grandma Rózia had eyes like two blue lakes and hands that could turn flour into gold. She sat in her favourite armchair, with a book of yellowed pages lying on her lap.

– Grandma, there won't be any rain – whispered Lena, jumping onto her lap. – What are we going to do?

Grandma stroked her hair.

– There is one way – she said mysteriously. – But you have to be brave.

That evening grandma told her about enchanted lands, about three little houses and about the Queen of Rain, who lived in a castle on the water. She taught her a song that opened doors:

_Drip, drip, drip, please open the door, to the Queen of Rain I'll be closer than before._

And then, from the pocket of her apron, she took out a silver key. She put it into the lock of the pantry, and the door opened onto another world — a meadow full of red poppies, crossed by a winding path.

– Go straight ahead – said grandma. – And remember: the answers are inside you.

---

The first little house was all yellow and looked like a giant banana. On its door there was a symbol of a red apple, and in front of it stood a guard — a tall pineapple with a bushy green hairdo. Lena sang the song, and the pineapple smiled and knocked on the door.

Inside it was like in a fairy tale about fruit. A chandelier made of grapes, a table made of a cut pear, chairs made of ruby pomegranate seeds, a sofa made of a soft orange. And by the stove stood an elderly lady in glasses, humming:

_Mhmm... I'm cutting my apples... Mhmm... I'll dust the flour off my apron..._

– I'm Aunt Stella – introduced herself the lady. – And you must be Rózia's granddaughter.

– How do you know? – Lena wondered.

– Because you have her eyes – answered Stella. – And her courage.

The aunt blindfolded Lena and handed her the first fruit. Lena touched it, smelled it, tasted it.

– It's a banana – she said with confidence.

The second one was heavy and round, and inside it hid a sweet, juicy treasure. A watermelon – she guessed.

The third one – tiny round balls joined in clusters. Grapes.

– You are as clever as your grandma – smiled Aunt Stella and gave her a piece of freshly baked apple pie. – Take this to Rózia. Tell her I miss her.

---

The second little house hid in a thicket of climbing plants, and on its door there was a symbol of a blue butterfly. The guard was a big, shaggy dog, who wagged his tail when Lena sang the song.

Inside it was like in a jungle. Parrots, toucans, hummingbirds flew overhead, monkeys jumped on the lianas, and little wild cats played among the green grass. Suddenly a small blue butterfly landed on Lena's nose.

– Follow me – he whispered.

He led her to a golden armchair, on which he sat with the grace of a king. It turned out that he was the guardian of this little house.

– Three riddles await you – he said. – Guess the sounds of the animals.

The first sound: _cock-a-doodle-doo!_

– A rooster! – called out Lena. – He's my morning alarm clock.

The second one: _moo!_

– Mucza! My cow!

The third was the hardest: a quiet, nightly chirping.

– Crickets – said Lena with a smile. – Every evening we listen to them with my parents, looking at the stars.

– Bravo! – called out the butterfly. – Your grandma would be proud.

He gave her a warm, white coat with a hood and a necklace with the words "friends forever".

– Give this to Rózia – he said. – And tell her I still think of her.

---

The third little house stood on a snowy hill and danced now to the right, now to the left, as if it moved to the rhythm of music. From its chimney, instead of smoke, black little notes rose up. On the door there was a symbol of a harp, and the guard was a tall drum, which started dancing when Lena sang.

Inside everything was covered in warm, soft snow, like down. By the harp sat a beautiful lady with long golden hair, dressed in a white lace gown.

– I'm Snowflake – she said, and her voice sounded like little bells. – And you must be little Lena.

– How do you know?

– Because your grandma told me about you. Sit down, I have tasks for you.

Lena repeated rhythms, imitated melodies, learned to play the silver flute. Every task ended in success.

– You are ready to meet the Queen – said Snowflake. – But first I found out that Kropeia's dress has frozen. You must thaw it by playing the flute.

Snowflake gave Lena silver bells for grandma.

– Tell her I miss her – she whispered.

---

The castle of the Queen of Rain stood in the middle of a huge lake. Lena reached it by boat and opened the gate with a symbol of a raindrop. Inside it was empty, but from one of the chambers came the sound of voices.

Queen Kropeia stood motionless in the middle of the hall, her dress of little drops had turned into ice. Clouds circled around her, helpless.

– Help me – asked the Queen.

Lena took out her flute and played the melody that Snowflake had taught her. The drops on the dress began to melt, returning to their liquid form. Kropeia breathed a sigh of relief and grabbed Lena by the hand.

– Thank you! – she called out. – Now I will help you.

They jumped onto a white cloud, and thousands of others followed them. They flew over Lena's parched land, and the Queen sang the song of rain:

_Rain, pour down on this land! Rain, pour down, pour down now!_

The first drops struck the thirsty earth. Then more. And more. Until the whole world around turned green.

People came out of their houses, laughed, danced in the rain. Lena hugged the Queen.

– This is all thanks to you – said Kropeia. – And I have a present for you. But open it tomorrow.

---

In the evening Lena came back to grandma. She told her everything, handed over the presents — the apple pie from Aunt Stella, the necklace from the butterfly, the bells from Snowflake. And grandma, instead of being surprised, smiled mysteriously and played on the bells the melody that Lena had learned in the snowy little house.

– How do you know that melody? – wondered Lena.

– Because Snowflake is my cousin – said grandma. – And the butterfly is my best friend from childhood. Aunt Stella is my aunt. And the Queen of Rain... is my sister.

– Your sister?!

– Yes. I used to be the Queen of Wind. But I fell in love with your grandpa and moved here, into the human world. And I have never regretted it.

Lena looked at grandma with her mouth open. Her grandma, who taught her to sew and to bake apple pie, was a queen of wind.

– And so – said grandma, taking out the silver key – maybe you'd like to go back there someday?

– Very much!

And then, in the glow of the setting sun, grandma and granddaughter opened the pantry door, behind which the enchanted land was waiting again. And they knew it was not the end of the adventures. It was only the beginning.

**END**
