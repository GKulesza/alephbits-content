**SYNCHRONICITY**

## I. THE BEGINNING

Sebastian Kowalski was thirty-two years old and had just lost eight thousand złoty.

He sat in his apartment in Warsaw's Mokotów district, staring at a computer screen on which a red line of a chart was blinking. It was past eleven in the evening, and for two hours he had been trying to understand what had gone wrong.

— It's impossible — he said to the empty room.

Nobody answered him. The apartment was quiet, except for the hum of the fridge and the distant wail of an ambulance on the street. Sebastian pushed his chair away from the desk and rested his head against the wall. For the last three months he had been consistently losing money on cryptocurrencies. First small amounts – a hundred, two hundred złoty – which he justified as "the cost of learning". Then bigger ones came. And today, in a single day, he had lost eight thousand.

He was a mathematics teacher at a Warsaw high school. He earned four thousand net. Eight thousand was two months of work, which had just dissolved into digital space.

He took a deep breath. He got up and went to the kitchen, where notes lay on the counter – charts, calculations, technical analyses that he had prepared over the last weeks. He looked at them and felt sick. They all pointed to an increase. They all told him he should buy.

And the market had fallen.

— Why? — he whispered.

Somewhere deep in the apartment, in the living room, lay a book he had bought a week earlier in a second-hand bookshop – "The Teachings of Don Juan" by Carlos Castaneda. He had picked it up by chance, because, like him, he needed answers. He bought it for ten złoty, read the first chapters and put it away. It was too... strange. Too magical. As a mathematics teacher, as a rationalist, he considered it a waste of time.

Now, with an empty account and a pain in his chest, he walked over to the shelf and took down the book. He opened it at a random page:

_"For a warrior there are no impossible things, only difficult ones."_

He rolled his eyes. But he did not put the book down.

---
## II. THE DEVELOPMENT

The next day Sebastian entered the classroom at eight in the morning with the feeling that someone had poured a bucket of cold water over his head. His students, thirty teenagers in school uniforms, looked at him with a mixture of boredom and expectation.

— Good morning — he said. — Today we'll continue the topic of quadratic functions.

He looked at the blackboard, on which someone had left a drawing – a sun with a smiling face. He should have erased it, but he didn't have the strength.

He began the lecture. He talked about zeros of functions, about the discriminant, about formulas. But his mind was elsewhere – with the red numbers on the screen, with the falling candles, with the feeling that the world was slipping out of his control.

— Sir? — he heard a voice from the last desk.

It was Lena, his best student. She was sixteen, had blue hair and an infuriating ability to ask questions that threw him off balance.

— Yes?

— Do quadratic functions always have a solution?

— No — he answered automatically. — If the delta is negative, there are no real solutions. That means the function never crosses the x-axis.

Lena nodded, as if that were the most interesting sentence she had ever heard in her life.

— It's like in life — she said. — Sometimes we look for answers where there are none. And we think it's our fault that we can't find them. But actually... there simply are no solutions.

Sebastian froze. For a moment he looked at his student, who smiled mysteriously, as if she knew something he didn't.

— What do you mean? — he asked.

— Well, for example... — Lena shrugged. — You try to control something that can't be controlled. And you think it's your fault it doesn't work out. But actually what you're trying to do is by definition impossible. Like looking for the solution to an equation with a negative delta.

Sebastian felt himself go cold. Not because Lena was right – but because she didn't know about his loss, about his nightly struggles with cryptocurrency charts. And yet she had hit the nail on the head.

— How... — he began, but Lena had already returned to her notebook, as if nothing had happened.

---

After the lesson Sebastian went to the staff room. It was empty, most teachers had classes at that time. He sat down at the desk and opened his laptop. He wanted to check what was happening on the market, but instead he opened the browser window and typed: "astrology and trading".

He didn't know why. Maybe it was Lena's question. Maybe that Castaneda book. Maybe desperation.

There were plenty of results. Pages about the phases of the Moon, about the retrograde of Mercury, about how planets influence financial decisions. Sebastian scrolled quickly, with the feeling that he was doing something stupid.

But one sentence stopped him:

_"Don't try to control the market. It's like trying to control waves. A surfer never controls the waves – he only uses their energy. If you fight the wave, you lose. If you ride with it, you win."_

He read it three times.

Then he closed the laptop and looked out the window. It was raining outside. Drops hit the pane, ran down, forming patterns that reminded him of dots on a chart. But this time he didn't see failure in them. He saw something else – a rhythm.

---

A week later Sebastian sat in a café on Nowy Świat. In front of him lay the book "Astrology for Sceptics", which he had bought in a bookshop two streets away. He drank black coffee and tried to understand how a tool he had never believed in could help him understand why the market behaves the way it does.

— Excuse me, is this seat free?

He looked up. In front of him stood a woman around forty – short red hair, glasses with thick frames, a smile that was both warm and attentive.

— Yes, of course — he said, pushing the book aside.

The woman sat down, ordered tea and for a moment looked at him with curiosity.

— Astrology for Sceptics — she read the title. — An interesting choice. Usually sceptics don't read books about astrology. And if they do, it's to debunk them.

— And you? — he asked. — Are you a sceptic?

The woman smiled.

— My name is Maria. I'm a psychologist. And an astrologer. And yes, I was a sceptic. For a long time.

Sebastian felt that what was happening was strangely familiar. As if something or someone was leading him in this direction.

— What made you stop being a sceptic?

Maria looked at him for a moment, as if weighing whether it was worth answering.

— The fact that my mind was interested in astrology from the start only in order to debunk it. To prove it doesn't work. In my studies I wrote a paper about astrology being a self-fulfilling prophecy. And in that process I discovered something I hadn't expected: that this tool makes sense. That it works. I don't know how, I don't know why – but it works.

Sebastian was silent. In his head appeared the memory from a week earlier, when Lena had told him about quadratic functions without solutions. About trying to control something that can't be controlled.

— I recently lost a lot of money — he said, though he didn't know why. — On cryptocurrencies. I tried to analyse the market, I made charts, simulations. And I thought that if I tried hard enough, I would understand everything and start winning. And meanwhile...
— Meanwhile it doesn't work — Maria finished. — Because you're trying to control the waves.

— How do you...

— Because we all do it. We want to have influence over reality. We want the world to arrange itself according to our plans. And when it doesn't, we feel lost. Hurt. — She paused, sipping her tea. — In astrology there is such a concept: the return of Saturn. It's a moment in life, usually around the age of 30, when everything we've built falls apart. And it's not a punishment. It's a gift. A chance to build something anew – on your own foundations.

Sebastian felt that he had just heard something that was the answer to a question he didn't know he was asking. Not about the market, not about money – about something deeper. Something that had been tormenting him for years.

— What should I do? — he asked.

Maria looked at him with kindness.

— Stop trying to control the waves. Start observing them. Understand their rhythm. And learn to surf them.

---
## III. THE TURNING POINT

Three months later Sebastian got up at five in the morning, as he had done every day for two weeks. He poured himself coffee, opened his laptop and checked the alignment of the planets. According to the app he had installed on Maria's recommendation, Mercury was just coming out of retrograde, and Venus was entering a trine with Jupiter – an alignment that, as he had been told, favoured making financial decisions.

He didn't believe in it. Not entirely. But something in him had begun to change.

For the last three months he hadn't traded. Only observed. Read. Learned. He tried to understand that the market is not an opponent to be defeated, but a living organism whose rhythm can be read. Like waves. Like planets. Like something that eludes control, but does not elude understanding.

That morning, for the first time in three months, he opened a position.

Not a big one. Only a symbolic one – a hundred złoty. But he did it consciously, calmly, without fever in his eyes.

He sat before the screen and watched the price rise. Then fall. Then rise again. And he felt no fear.

— Fine — he said to himself. — I don't know how this will end. But I know it's just a wave.

He closed the position after two hours with a profit of seven złoty. It was a ridiculous amount. But he wasn't looking at the money. He was looking at something else – at the calm that had appeared in his head.

He took the Castaneda book from the shelf and opened it at the page he had marked:

_"A warrior takes responsibility for his decisions, but not for the results. The results belong to infinity."_

He smiled. That was exactly what he had needed to hear.

---
## IV. THE ENDING

In December, when the first snow fell in Warsaw, Sebastian went for a walk along the Vistula. In his pocket he had a phone showing the astrology app – information that Mars had just entered the sign of Leo, which was supposed to mean the beginning of a new cycle of action.

He didn't believe in it blindly. But he had learned to accept that there are things he doesn't understand. And that it's all right.

His trading account was slightly in the red – but not with the kind of red that destroyed him. It was a minus he knew how to live with. He knew his strategy, his limits, his rules. He had stopped fighting the market. He had begun to observe it.

He stopped on the bridge, leaned on the railing and looked down at the icy water. Somewhere in the distance someone was playing music – an old hit from the 90s, which he associated with his childhood. For a moment he thought of his mother, who had died when he was twenty. Of his father, who had left a year later. Of how long he had tried to understand why it happened, why the world is so unfair.

And then he understood something that came to him like a revelation: he didn't have to understand. He only had to accept.

As Maria had said – astrology is not a verdict, it's navigation. It doesn't tell you what will happen. It tells you when it's a good time to act, and when it's better to let go. And that's enough.

He took out his phone and wrote her a message:

_"Today, for the first time in a year, I closed the month in the black. Not by much. But in the black. Thank you."_

A few minutes later the answer came:

_"It's not me. It's you. You trusted yourself."_

He put the phone back in his pocket and moved on. The snow fell on his face, cold and refreshing. He thought of his students, who would come to the mathematics lesson tomorrow, and whom he taught not only formulas, but something more – that in life there are equations without solutions. And that this is not a failure. It's just a hint to look for answers elsewhere.

He remembered Lena's words, which he had heard a month earlier, when he had stopped her after the lesson:

_"Sir, what are you doing now to be happier?"_

He hadn't answered then. He didn't know. But now, standing on the bridge, looking at the river flowing in its own rhythm, he knew.

_"I stopped fighting"_ – he thought. _"And I started flowing."_

He took a deep breath and set off towards home.
