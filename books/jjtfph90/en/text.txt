**DEAD EYES**

## I. THE BEGINNING

Kasia knew something was wrong before she even opened her eyes.

She lay in bed in Karol's apartment, hearing his steady breathing beside her. Her eyes were closed, but she felt him looking at her. Not with the warm gaze with which he used to wake her in the morning, but with something else. Something that made her skin break out in goosebumps.

She opened her eyes.

Karol was lying on his side, propped on his elbow, looking at her. He was smiling. A beautiful smile that on their first date had made her feel special. But now, in the moonlight, his eyes were empty. Dead. As if he were looking at her through a pane of glass behind which there was no one.

— You were asleep — he said. His voice was warm, but there was no warmth in his eyes.

— Karol... what's going on?

— Nothing. — He smiled wider. — I'm just looking at you. You're beautiful when you sleep.

Kasia tried to smile, but she couldn't. A memory from a week earlier appeared in her mind, when her friend, a psychologist, had warned her:

*"Watch out for men who are too perfect. Who talk about love too quickly. Who want to get to know you too quickly. Who..."*

— I need to go to the bathroom — she said, getting up from the bed.

In the bathroom she locked the door and looked in the mirror. In the reflection she saw a woman who, for the past three months, had believed she had found love. Karol was everything she had been looking for — handsome, charming, ambitious. He spoke to her like no one ever had. He gave her so much attention that she felt like a queen.

But recently something had changed. Small criticisms began. Then bigger ones. Then came that look she couldn't name.

She took out her phone and texted her friend:

_"I have a feeling he has no soul. Is that possible?"_

The answer came after a few seconds:

_"Listen to your intuition. And watch his eyes. Everything shows in the eyes."_

Kasia went back to the bedroom. Karol was still looking at her.

— Everything all right? — he asked.

— Yes — she lied. — Everything's all right.

---

## II. THE DEVELOPMENT

A week later Kasia sat in a café with Karol. It was Saturday, the sun was shining, and he was talking about his work, his plans, his vision of the future. He spoke beautifully. But when she looked at him, she saw only the surface. As if she were talking to an actor who plays a role but doesn't feel what he says.

— What do you think about love? — she asked suddenly.

Karol looked at her. For a fraction of a second she saw something in his eyes that froze her — not surprise, not reflection, but _calculation_. As if he were weighing which answer would bring him the most benefit.

— Love is the most beautiful feeling in the world — he said, taking her hand. — I feel it when I'm with you. Every moment with you is like... like we were soaring a million meters above the earth.

The words were perfect. Too perfect. Kasia remembered a conversation with a psychologist she had seen in a podcast:

_"If someone talks about love like poetry, but there is nothing in his eyes... it means he has learned the words, but he doesn't know the melody."*

— Karol — she said quietly. — Do you even feel anything?

Silence fell. Karol let go of her hand. His face didn't change — it was still the same mask he had worn since their first meeting. But in his eyes... she saw something she would never forget.

For a fraction of a second, the mask slipped.

She saw emptiness. Absolute, cold emptiness.

— What do you mean? — he asked, and his voice was already different. Not warm. Not charming. Just... empty.

— Nothing — she answered, standing up. — I have to go.

— Kasia, sit down.

— No.

She walked out of the café without looking back. She felt his gaze on her back — heavy, as if someone were placing invisible weights on her shoulders. Only when she was a few streets away did she catch her breath.

The phone rang. Karol.

She answered.

— Kasia, I don't understand what happened. I'm sorry if I did something. It's just... I love you so much that sometimes...

— Stop — she interrupted him. — Stop saying you love me. You don't know what that means.

He was silent for a moment. And then he said something that made her blood run cold:

— You know, I always thought you were different. But you're just like all the others. Emotional. Weak. You don't understand that what I'm doing, I'm doing for us.

— For us? — she laughed bitterly. — Karol, you don't have any "us". You only have yourself.

She hung up.

---

## III. THE TURNING POINT

A month later Kasia returned to her apartment. She was alone, but for the first time in a long time she felt that this was good. Instead of feeling emptiness, she felt peace.

For the past four weeks she had read everything she could find about psychopathy. About cognitive and affective empathy. About superficial charm. About how easy it is to be taken in by words that sound like love but are only a tool.

And then she understood.

Karol wasn't a bad person. He was someone who simply had no access to what she called humanity. For him, love was a strategy. A relationship — a game. And she was a pawn that had stopped being useful.

She sat on the couch and opened the notebook in which she wrote down her thoughts. On the first page there was a sentence she remembered from a podcast about psychopathy:

_"Psychopathic people perceive emotionality as weakness."*

Underneath she added:

_"But that doesn't mean we are weak. It means they don't know what strength is."*

She closed the notebook and looked out the window. It was raining outside, but she felt that inside, it was finally dry.

She picked up her phone and sent Karol one last message:

_"I don't know if you'll ever feel what I feel. But I know I don't want to be part of your game anymore. Goodbye."*

She sent it. She blocked the number. And for the first time in months, she felt she could breathe.

---

## IV. THE ENDING

A year later Kasia ran workshops for women who had survived toxic relationships. She told them about psychopathy, about manipulation, about how easy it is to be taken in by words that are only an instrument.

— Remember — she said to a room full of women — if someone talks to you like from a book, but there's nothing in his eyes... run. Trust your intuition. It knows what your mind doesn't want to accept yet.

After the workshop, a woman with tearful eyes approached her.

— I had a man like that too — she said. — I thought I was going crazy. That it was my fault, that I couldn't accept love. And now I know... it wasn't love. It was a project.

Kasia took her hand.

— You're not alone — she said. — And you never were.

She left the room and looked at the sky. She knew that somewhere in the city, Karol was still looking for his next victim. Still using the same words, the same mask. But no longer on her.

And that was the most important victory.

---

## V. THE EPILOGUE

A few years later Kasia met Karol by chance on the street. He was with another woman — younger, smiling, gazing at him as if he were a god. Karol saw Kasia and for a moment his mask trembled. Something appeared in his eyes that she hadn't seen in years — a shadow of frustration.

— Kasia! — he called out, approaching. — How are you? Long time no see.

— Fine — she answered calmly. — And you?

— Great. This is Ola, my girlfriend.

Ola smiled radiantly. Kasia looked at her and felt something in her heart she couldn't name. Compassion? Fear? Or maybe simply sadness that someone else was about to go through the same thing she had.

— Nice to meet you — she said to Ola. — Watch out for him. He has cold eyes.

Ola looked at her in surprise. Karol laughed, but Kasia saw a glint in his eyes — cold, calculating.

— I'm joking — she added quickly. — It was nice seeing you. Bye.

She turned and walked her own way. She felt his gaze on her back — the same one she remembered from that night. But this time she wasn't afraid.

She knew she had won.

Not because she had beaten him. But because she had stopped being his pawn.
