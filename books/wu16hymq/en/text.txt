**THE POT ON THE HEAD**

## I. THE BEGINNING

Marek woke up at 3:33 in the morning, not remembering what he had dreamed. He lay in the darkness of his apartment on Long Island, hearing the hum of the old radiator and the distant wail of sirens. Outside the window, Montauk was sunk in night silence, but he felt that something was wrong.

His hand, completely unconsciously, reached for the bedside table. He took out a pot. An old, enamel one, with a chipped piece of enamel on the rim. He put it on his head.

— What are you doing? — he whispered to himself.

He didn't know. But he felt that it was important. That when the pot was on his head, something changed. His thoughts became clearer. As if someone was cutting off a signal that normally drowned out his mind.

For as long as he could remember, he had nightmares. Not the ordinary kind — about falling or being chased. Nightmares about light, about sound, about the feeling that someone was rummaging around in his head. He woke up drenched in sweat, with a pain in his temples, with the impression that he had lost something important.

His wife said it was stress. The doctor prescribed him sleeping pills. But Marek knew it wasn't stress. It was something else. Something that had happened before he was even born.

---
## II. THE DEVELOPMENT

A week later, Marek stood before the abandoned gate of Camp Hero. The forest was dense, dark, and the paths leading to the main building were overgrown with weeds. In his hand he held a book he had found by chance in a second-hand bookshop — "The Montauk Project: Experiments in Time" by Preston B. Nichols.

He read it in one sitting. And with every page he felt something inside him cracking.

_"Nichols describes himself as an electrical engineer who, in the 1970s, became interested in metaphysics..."_

Marek was an engineer. An electrical one, too.

_"He noticed something very strange. All the people he worked with experienced, at the same time, a kind of mental block..."_

Marek closed the book and looked at the gate. He knew he shouldn't go in there. But he felt he had to.

He squeezed through a gap in the fence. The forest was dense, quiet. Somewhere in the distance a bird was singing. Marek walked on, not knowing exactly where he was heading. Until suddenly he found himself in front of something he hadn't expected to see.

The entrance to an underground complex. Blocked by heavy steel doors, covered in rust and ivy.

— It's impossible — he whispered. — It's just a theory.

But his hands, again unconsciously, reached into his pocket. He took out a key. An old, brass one, which he had found in a drawer after his father's death. He didn't know what it fitted. Until now.

He put the key in the lock. He turned it. The doors opened with a dull, metallic creak.

---
## III. THE TURNING POINT

Inside it was dark. Marek switched on the torch on his phone and set off down the corridor. The walls were concrete, damp, covered with writings he didn't understand. He walked deeper and deeper, until he reached a room that stopped him in his tracks.

A chair. Old, metal, with wires connected to frayed cables. Exactly the kind Nichols had described.

Marek felt himself go cold. Not because of the temperature. Because of the memories that suddenly began to flood back.

He saw himself. Young, maybe ten years old, sitting in that chair. He saw people in white coats saying something to him. He heard voices ordering him to close his eyes and "think of something that doesn't exist".

— It's not true — he whispered. — It's impossible.

But his body remembered. His hands remembered the touch of the wires. His head remembered the pain. And that strange, metallic taste in his mouth, which appeared whenever he tried to remember.

He fell to his knees. An image appeared in his mind — a tunnel of light, an open portal, something that looked like a Demogorgon, but worse. It was real.

And then he heard a voice.

— You're back.

He turned around. In the doorway stood an elderly man, leaning on a cane. He had a pot on his head.

— Who are you? — asked Marek.

— Someone who also forgot. And someone who has finally remembered everything. — The man stepped closer. — My name is Preston. And you, Marek... you were one of us.

---
## IV. THE ENDING

They sat in a small room that had once been an office. Preston took the pot off his head and put it on the table.

— This is no joke — he said. — These pots protect against a suppressing field. The government used it to control people in the area. So that nobody would remember what happened here.

— And what happened here? — asked Marek.

— Everything you read in the book. And more. — Preston sighed. — We were children, Marek. They kidnapped us, subjected us to experiments. They amplified our psychic abilities. They used us to open portals. And then... they erased our memory.

Marek felt like crying.

— Why do I remember? — he asked.

— Because your father was one of us too. And before he died, he left you the key. And the pot. And the book. — Preston looked him in the eye. — He wanted you to come back. To remember who you really are.

Marek was silent for a long moment. Then he stood up and walked over to the chair. He touched it with trembling hands.

— What should I do? — he asked.

— What we always did. Discover the truth. And tell the world about it. — Preston smiled sadly. — But be careful. They're still here. And they don't want anyone to remember.

Marek took the pot from the table and put it on his head.

— I remember — he said. — Now I remember everything.

---
## V. THE EPILOGUE

A year later, Marek published a book. Not one like Nichols's — not full of fantasy and conspiracy theories. A true one, based on the documents he had found in the underground of Camp Hero. On the recordings he had discovered in forgotten safes. On the accounts of other people who also remembered.

The book did not become a bestseller. Too many people didn't want to believe it. But for Marek that didn't matter.

Because he knew he had finally done what he had to do. He had remembered who he was. And he hadn't let anyone take that away from him.

Sometimes at night, as he lay down to sleep, he heard the same voice as back then in the underground. But now he wasn't afraid of it. Now he knew it was only an echo of a past that had finally stopped ruling him.

He fell asleep with the pot on his head. And for the first time in years, he had no nightmares.

**THE END**
