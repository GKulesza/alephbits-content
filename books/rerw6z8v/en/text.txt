**FRANEK AND LITTLE KAROLEK**

In a certain tenement house on a cobblestone lane that led to the old market square, extraordinary things happened. The house was beautiful – it had tall wooden windows, heavy wrought-iron doors and a lovely little garden tended by Mrs Gienia, who lived in the annexe with the laundry room. Across the way, Mrs Celina ran an ironing shop, and the two friends knew everything about every resident.

On the first floor lived Miss Gertrude, who adored throwing parties and slept until noon. Next door lived Mr Hieronim – an elderly gentleman who was deaf in his left ear, which was actually a blessing during Miss Gertrude's noisy parties. In the basement lived a young widow with her little son Karolek, and at the other end of the courtyard the caretaker Michał with his wife and son.

And it was precisely that son – Franek – who was the terror of the whole tenement house.

---
Franek was ten years old, with eyes like two sparks and a smile that was always plotting something. There wasn't a day when he didn't play a trick on someone. Once he hid Mrs Gienia's watering can, and while she searched the whole laundry room for it, he put it back on the doorstep so she would trip over it. Another time he stuck a pine cone in the tail of Miss Gertrude's poodle – a fat, lazy poodle – and then brought Mrs Celina's cat over, which flung itself at the poodle in a fury, setting off a real domestic war.

– It's that Franek again! – shouted Mrs Celina.

– It's his doing! – echoed Miss Gertrude.

And Franek just giggled, hiding behind the well in the courtyard.

The worst thing was that Franek couldn't learn. The letters escaped his head, and reading was so hard for him that he preferred to run out into the courtyard and look for new pranks. Franek's mother worried every day about what would become of him.

– Franek, when will you finally settle down? – she said, patching yet another torn shirt.

– Mum, I'll be good, I promise! – he answered, hugging her tightly.

But a moment later he was in Mrs Gienia's little garden again, on the lookout for a new chance for a prank.

---
One day sorrow settled over the tenement house. The young widow from the basement, who lived with little Karolek, fell seriously ill. The neighbours helped as best they could, but after a few weeks the woman died, leaving a tiny boy who had no one.

The residents of the house gathered to decide what to do with Karolek. Everyone made excuses – one had too much work, another had no proper conditions, and still another thought he was too old to care for an infant.

Franek stood with his parents and listened. Something squeezed his heart. He saw little Karolek, who lay in a neighbour's cradle and cried softly, as if he knew he had been left all alone in the world.

Suddenly Franek grabbed his mother's hand.

– Mum! – he called out. – Let's take him!

– What? Take who? – his mother was surprised.

– Karolek! Let's take Karolek into our home!

His mother looked at him as if he had gone mad.

– Franek, what are you saying? You yourself cause me nothing but trouble! And what about a little scrap like that?

– But Mum! – Franek threw himself on his knees before her. – Please! Let's take him! I'll change! I'll be good, I promise! If we take Karolek, I will never play a single prank again! I swear!

Just then Franek's father came in. Seeing his son on his knees, he asked:

– What's going on?

– Franek wants us to take Karolek – answered his mother.

His father looked at his son, then at little Karolek, who had just stopped crying and smiled with his toothless gums.

– Maybe that's not a bad idea – he said quietly. – Maybe that's just what we all need.

Franek didn't wait a moment. He shot out like a slingshot, ran to the basement, picked Karolek up in his arms and carried him into his own home. From that moment the little boy became part of their family.

---
And then something extraordinary began to happen. Franek changed as if touched by a magic wand.

Every morning he got up first and checked whether Karolek was cold. He fed him, combed his hair, bathed him, rocked him to sleep and sang him songs. When Karolek cried, Franek took him in his arms and walked around the room with him, telling him about all the wonderful things in the world.

– This is a window, Karolek – he said. – And outside the window is the sun. The sun shines in the sky and warms us all.

Karolek looked at him with big eyes and smiled.

Everyone in the house was amazed. Mrs Gienia couldn't believe that this was the same Franek who had once hidden her watering can. Now he watered her flowers, unasked. Miss Gertrude was in shock that Franek helped her carry her shopping up to the first floor. Mr Hieronim noticed that no one had broken his window in a long time.

– What have you done to that boy? – Mrs Celina asked Franek's mother.

– I did nothing – answered his mother. – It was Karolek who changed him.

---
One day, during a walk in the park, Franek was pushing Karolek's pram and telling him about everything they saw around them.

– You see, Karolek, that's a squirrel. It gathers nuts for winter. It hides them in a hollow tree, but it often forgets where it put them. Then new trees grow from the nuts. Clever, isn't it?

Karolek stretched out his little hand and pointed at a leaf that had fallen from the tree.

– That's a leaf, Karolek. It fell from the tree because autumn is here. The leaves turn yellow, orange and red. Then they fall to the ground to make room for new leaves in spring.

Karolek smiled and started to babble happily.

Then Franek looked at him seriously.

– Karolek – he said. – I'm your big brother. I will always take care of you. I promise you that I will be a good example for you. And that I'll teach you everything I know myself. And even what I don't know yet – because I'll learn it. For you.

Karolek reached out his little arms, and Franek took him into his arms and hugged him tightly.

---
That evening, when he came home, Franek saw his father sitting in the armchair with his pipe.

– Dad – he said shyly. – In September I'm starting school. I'd like to be able to read. So I can read books to Karolek. Will you teach me?

His father looked at him, moved.

– Of course, son – he answered. – Are you ready?

– Best right away – said Franek.

His father went to the shelf, took down an old primer and handed it to his son. Franek opened the book and began slowly, with difficulty, but with great determination, to join letters into words. His father helped him patiently, and Karolek lay nearby in his crib listening, falling asleep with a smile on his face.

Every evening they sat together: father, Franek and little Karolek. Franek read aloud, more and more fluently, and Karolek listened with his mouth open, as if he understood every word.

---
When September came, Franek started school. He was no longer the same boy who ran away from lessons and preferred to play tricks. Now he studied diligently, because he knew that in the evening a little brother was waiting for him who wanted to hear fairy tales.

And when he came home, he immediately took Karolek onto his lap, opened a book and read to him about squirrels, about chestnuts, about autumn leaves, about friendship, about love, about the fact that it's worth being good. And Karolek listened, and then fell asleep peacefully, feeling the warmth of his big brother.

The residents of the house couldn't get over their surprise. Of the former scamp not a trace remained. Now everyone spoke of him with respect: "Franek", not "Franek the rascal". And they knew that it was little Karolek who had made the boy learn what it means to love and to be responsible.

And Franek knew this was only the beginning. That there would be many more years, many books, many walks and many stories. And that love for his little brother had changed him forever, into the best brother Karolek could ever have dreamed of.

**THE END**
