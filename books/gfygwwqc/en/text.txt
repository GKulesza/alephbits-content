**BLITZKRIEG**

## I. THE BEGINNING

Ola was thirty-eight years old and had just come back, for the twelfth time this month, from a date that promised everything and gave nothing.

She sat in the taxi, looking at the flashing lights of Warsaw, and tried to remember what the last one's name was. Krzysztof? Karol? Or maybe Kamil? She had no idea. He had a nice smile, worked for a corporation, said he liked mountains and Italian food. And that was everything she knew about him. They had spent three hours together, and she had not even remembered his surname.

— You're so lucky — her colleague told her as she was leaving work. — So many dates, so many men. I wish I had your success.

Ola smiled wryly. "Success" — that was a good word for something she felt as desperation.

She went back to her empty flat, took off her shoes and sat down on the sofa. On the table lay the book she had bought a week earlier — "On a First Date You Can See Everything" by Magdalena Chorzewska. She opened it at a random page and read:

_"If you hear on a date that all the exes were bad, you will be the next one who is bad."_

She laughed bitterly. All her ex-boyfriends said the same thing — their previous girlfriends were bad, did not understand them, did not appreciate them. And she, foolish as she was, thought she was different. That she would be the one who would understand them.

And it always ended the same way.

She stood up and walked over to the window. Behind the glass, Warsaw glittered in the night lights, and she felt she was about to burst into tears. Not because she was alone. But because she did not know how to stop repeating the same mistakes.

---

## II. THE DEVELOPMENT

The next day Ola went to therapy. To Magdalena Chorzewska, whose book she had been reading. They met in her office in Mokotów — a cosy place full of plants and soft light.

— Tell me about the last date — said Magda, sitting down opposite her.

Ola sighed.

— It was... all right. Nice men. Work, mountains, Italian food. A normal man.

— And what did you feel?

— Nothing. — Ola shrugged. — Or... I don't know. I thought that if I stayed longer, I would feel something. But I didn't.

Magda nodded.

— How many times has it been?

— I don't know. — Ola laughed nervously. — Ninety? A hundred? I used to count, but I stopped.

— And what are you looking for?

Ola was silent for a moment. A memory appeared in her head — a history lecture she had watched on television a few days earlier. They talked about the Third Reich, about how the Germans started the war having ammunition reserves for only six weeks. About how they were afraid of peace.

— I don't know — she said quietly. — Maybe I'm just... afraid to stop looking.

— Like the Germans in 1939 — Magda smiled.

Ola looked at her in astonishment.

— What?

— You mentioned you had watched a lecture about the Second World War — said Magda. — I remember the passage where they said the Third Reich was afraid not of war, but of peace. That the system was built on constant expansion, and stopping meant catastrophe. — She paused and looked Ola straight in the eye. — Isn't that exactly what you're doing? Every date is a new campaign for you, a new conquest. And you're afraid that if you stop, you'll have to face the emptiness.

Ola felt a chill run through her.

— It's not like that...

— Then how? — Magda leaned forward. — Tell me, Ola, when were you last alone for a longer time? Not on dates, not searching. Simply alone.

— I... — Ola pressed her lips together. — I don't remember.

— Exactly. — Magda sat back more comfortably. — You know, in psychology we talk about something called relational hunger. When a person is so thirsty for contact that they lose the ability to tell good from bad. To ignore red flags. To listen to themselves.

Ola lowered her eyes.

— I'm afraid that if I stop, no one will find me.

— And what if he already has? — asked Magda. — But you don't see him, because you're too busy looking.

---

## III. THE TURNING POINT

A week later Ola sat in a café on Nowy Świat. She had arranged a date — another one, the thirteenth that month. But this time she did something different. Before leaving, she wrote on a piece of paper:

_"What am I looking for? Who am I? What do I not want to repeat?"_

She read it three times. Then she put the paper into her bag and left.

The man who was waiting for her at the café was called Tomasz. He was tall, with dark hair and warm eyes. He worked as an architect, liked reading books and hiking in the mountains — but he did not talk about it in a way that sounded like a rehearsed formula.

They sat at the table, talking about work, about travel, about favourite books. But Ola listened differently than usual. Instead of looking for confirmation that he was "the one", she looked for the truth about him.

— And what are your relationships with your exes like? — she asked at one point.

Tomasz looked at her with surprise, but he did not avoid the question.

— It varies — he said. — I don't get along with one, but I have good contact with another. She's the mother of my child, so we have to talk.

— And you don't hold a grudge against her?

— I did. For the first year. — He smiled sadly. — But I understood it made no sense. We were together, then we stopped being together. That doesn't mean she's bad. It means we were bad for each other.

Ola felt something crack inside her. All her life she had heard from men that their exes were bad, that it was their fault, that they did not understand them. And she believed it. But now, listening to Tomasz, she understood that it was not true. Or at least — that it did not always have to be true.

— Why is it so easy for us to ignore red flags? — she asked, more to herself than to him.

Tomasz looked at her for a moment.

— Because we're afraid that if we see them, we'll have to admit that we've made a mistake. And admitting a mistake is harder than enduring it for years.

Ola felt she was about to cry. Not from sadness, but from relief. That someone had finally said it.

— For the last ten years I've been looking for someone to save me — she said quietly. — And now I think the only person I could have saved was myself.

Tomasz did not answer. He simply took her hand and squeezed it.

— This is not a date — he said. — This is the beginning of something else.

— Of what?

— I don't know. — He smiled. — But I'd like to find out.

---

## IV. THE CONCLUSION

A year had passed. Ola and Tomasz were together — not as a couple that "tries", but as two people who had consciously decided to be with each other. It was not perfect. There were arguments, misunderstandings, days when they did not know whether it made sense. But they were together.

And then Ola understood something she had not understood for years. That a relationship is not a goal. It is a process. That it is not about finding someone who will be perfect, but about finding someone with whom you can be on the way.

She took Magdalena Chorzewska's book and opened it at the last page, where she had written a note:

_"The best relationships are not born on the first date. They are born after the third, the fifth, the tenth. They are born when you stop looking for the ideal and start looking for a human being."_

She smiled and closed the book.

The sun was shining outside the window. Tomasz was in the kitchen, making coffee. Somewhere in the background music was playing — she did not know it, but it had something familiar in it, a rhythm that reminded her that everything has its time.

She stood up and walked over to him.

— I love you — she said.

— I know — he answered. — And I love you.

She hugged him and thought about everything she had been through. About the hundreds of dates, about the red flags she had ignored, about the men who promised everything and gave nothing. And about that one man who promised nothing and gave everything.

_"Blitzkrieg"_ — she thought. _"Lightning war. And as it turns out, the Germans in 1939 were not the only ones who needed it. I did too. Until I finally understood that true peace is not about winning. It is about stopping. About being."_

She took a deep breath and allowed herself to be.

Simply to be.

---

## V. EPILOGUE

A few months later Ola ran workshops about dating for women who felt just as lost as she had. She told them about red flags, about ignoring signals, about how easy it is to fool yourself. But she also talked about something else — about the fact that dating is not a goal. That the goal is to get to know yourself.

— Remember — she said to a room full of women — that a first date shows a lot. But only if you are ready to see it. If you are not, you will only see what you want to see. And that is the biggest trap.

She looked at the book that lay on the lectern — "On a First Date You Can See Everything". She smiled.

— For ten years I saw nothing. And then I saw everything.

The room answered with applause. And Ola thought about Tomasz, who was waiting for her at home. About how many years she had looked for someone who would understand her, before she understood that she had to understand herself first.

_"Blitzkrieg"_ — she thought again. _"Sometimes you have to go through war to appreciate peace."_

She left the hall and headed home.

The sun was shining outside the window. And everything was all right.
