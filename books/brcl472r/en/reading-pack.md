## Text

**THE BEAR**

Kuba always said that the worst thing about arguments with Marta was not the words. Not what they said — although that hurt too. The worst part was that moment when they both already knew that it was about to explode. When the silence between them thickened, and he looked at her back turned away in bed and felt something invisible tightening around his throat.

This time it started with the dishwasher. As usual.

– You didn't empty it again – said Marta, coming into the kitchen. She wasn't shouting. She didn't even raise her voice. But Kuba heard everything in that sentence: tiredness, disappointment, the quiet resignation of someone who had already asked for the same thing hundreds of times.

– I was going to empty it after dinner – he answered, not taking his eyes off his phone. – I forgot. I'll do it in a minute.

– It's not about the dishwasher.

That was when he should have put the phone down. He knew it. He knew those four words meant: "It's about us. About the fact that you're not here, even when you are. About the fact that for months we've only talked about the dishwasher, the shopping and the bills, and when I try to talk about something more, you always have something to do."

But he didn't put it down. Instead he said:

– Then what is it about?

And that was the moment when everything collapsed.

---
Two hours later they were sitting at opposite ends of the apartment. He in the living room, she in the bedroom. Between them – silence thicker than ever. Kuba was scrolling through his phone aimlessly until he came across a recording. A podcast he had been subscribed to for a long time. The title contained a name he recognized – Tomasz Kammel. He used to watch him on television, then started listening to his talks about communication. He wasn't sure whether he had the strength for it now. But he didn't have the strength for anything else either.

He pressed play.

Kammel was talking about arguments. About the fact that the worst thing you can do is let contempt into a relationship – that fourth horseman of the apocalypse, as John Gottman called it. That when you say "you're hopeless" instead of "what you're saying is foolish", you're not just hurting the behaviour – you're hurting the person. That when you say "you always" and "you never", you're building a cage with no way out.

And then he told them about the bear.

Kuba listened as Kammel explained: when an argument slips out of control, when you're both so wound up that words you can't take back are about to be said, you have to shout "bear". Not "teddy bear" – a teddy bear is cute and inadequate. Bear. And then you both have to stop. Come closer to each other. Hug. Stay like that for a moment. And then, if you want, you can go on arguing – but you won't be the same anymore.

Kuba turned off the phone. For a moment he sat in silence, looking at the dark screen. Then he got up and went to the bedroom.

Marta was lying on the bed, facing the wall. She wasn't asleep – he could hear her breathing, uneven, broken, as if she had just been crying. He stood in the doorway.

– Marta?

Nothing.

– I wanted to... – he trailed off. He didn't know how to say it. Everything that came to mind sounded stupid. But he remembered what Kammel had said about communicating emotions. That instead of "you're spiteful" it's better to say "I feel hurt when you talk to me like that".

– When you say it's not about the dishwasher, and you walk away... – he began, trying to keep his voice calm – I feel like I'm letting you down. Every day. And I don't know how to fix it.

Silence. Then Marta turned around slowly.

– I don't know either – she said quietly. – But we could try.

That night they didn't find all the answers. But for the first time in months Kuba didn't sleep alone – even when they lay in silence, it was a different kind of silence. The kind that doesn't divide, but waits.

The next day, when he came back from work, there was a note stuck to the fridge. Large, crooked letters: "If we argue, I shout BEAR."

Kuba smiled. Underneath he added: "And you hug me. No matter what."

He didn't know whether it would work. But for the first time in a long time he felt like finding out.

**THE END**
