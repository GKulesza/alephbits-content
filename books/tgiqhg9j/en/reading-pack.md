## Text

**READ MY MIND**


When Magda first saw Grzesiek in the café in Kazimierz, she thought it was impossible. Tall, handsome, wearing an elegant coat that emphasised his broad shoulders. He sat at a table by the window, drinking black coffee and reading a book – a real one, made of paper, not on a phone. Magda knew at once that this was the moment her life was about to change.

Over the following weeks they dated, went to the cinema, strolled along the Vistula, talked for hours about everything and nothing. Grzesiek was perfect – he listened when she spoke, remembered small details, brought her flowers for no reason. Magda felt she had finally found someone with whom she could be happy.

But then things started to get difficult.


**The first signal**

Grzesiek had a habit of opening the door for her when she came back from work. He stood in the hallway, smiled, took her in his arms and kissed her. Magda loved this ritual – she felt loved, important, noticed.

Until one evening she came back tired, stressed after a hard day. Grzesiek was also coming back from work – but Magda was busy with her computer, writing an important email, and did not look up when she heard the key in the door. She opened the door for him, but instead of hugging him, she went straight back to her desk.

Grzesiek said nothing. For the rest of the evening he was quiet, as if something was bothering him. Magda did not ask, because she had her own thoughts, her own work, her own problems. Only the next day, when she finally asked what was going on, she heard:

– I was hurt that you didn't greet me.

Magda looked at him in surprise.

– But I did open the door for you.

– Yes, but you didn't give me a kiss. You didn't hug me. You went straight back to the computer.

– I was busy – said Magda, feeling something begin to boil inside her. – I have the right to be tired.

– You do – replied Grzesiek. – But I also have the right to feel left out.

Magda did not answer. She knew he was right, but she did not want to admit it. She preferred to shift the blame onto him – that he was too sensitive, that he was exaggerating, that it was just a silly situation.

Deep inside, however, she felt that something was wrong. That she had made a mistake. But she could not fix it – because she could not say: "I'm sorry, next time I'll pay attention."


**The second signal**

A few weeks later Magda and Grzesiek quarrelled about something trivial – about the fact that Grzesiek had not bought milk, even though she had asked him three times. Or four. She no longer remembered exactly. She only remembered that she had told him about it, and he had forgotten.

– I explained it to you five times – she snapped, feeling irritation grow inside her like an avalanche. – Why should I say it a sixth time, if you won't remember anyway?

Grzesiek looked at her.

– I'm sorry, I forgot. That's all.

– You forgot – repeated Magda, filling the word with venom. – You always forget.

– Not always – he replied calmly. – But sometimes I do. That doesn't mean I don't listen. It means I'm human.

Magda did not want to hear that. She had already built the story in her head: he does not listen to her, he does not appreciate her, he does not do what he should. And yet she had explained it to him so many times. Why should she say it again?


**The third signal**

During a meeting of Kapci – that was the name of the support group Magda attended – she heard a story that shook her.

Karolina, who led the meeting, told of a girl who for years agreed to things she did not want. She pretended to like the mountains, though she preferred the sea. She pretended to like cooking, though she hated the kitchen. She pretended to be happy, though inside she felt emptiness. And one day she exploded. She made a scene, shouted, cried, accused her partner of not understanding her, not seeing her, not appreciating her.

And he looked at her in shock and asked: "But didn't you love the mountains? Didn't you love cooking? What changed?"

She answered: "I never loved the mountains. I was pretending to please you."

Magda felt her heart start to beat faster. She remembered how, at the beginning of the relationship, she had told Grzesiek that she loved long walks – though she preferred lying on the sofa. How she said she liked listening to jazz – though she preferred pop. How she pretended to be interested in his work, though she really did not understand half of what he said.

She had done it out of love. Out of a desire to please. Out of fear that if she was herself, he would leave her.

But now, listening to that story, she understood that pretending is not love. It is a trap that sooner or later explodes.


**The fourth signal**

Grzesiek had his flaws. He was forgetful, absent-minded at times, and sometimes it looked as if he was thinking about something else while she spoke. Magda had her flaws. She was oversensitive, got annoyed easily, and often assumed that he knew what she felt, even though she did not tell him.

And every day they made the same mistake: they assumed the other person knew.

Magda assumed that Grzesiek knew she needed a hug when she came back tired. Grzesiek assumed that Magda knew he needed appreciation when he cooked dinner. And both felt unappreciated, unloved, lonely – even though both loved each other sincerely.

Until one day Magda went to a Kapci meeting and heard words that changed everything:

– If you don't say something, it doesn't exist. If you expect someone to guess, you will wait all your life.

Magda came home and sat down with Grzesiek in the living room. It was after an argument – about the fact that he had not taken her out for dinner, even though she had had a bad day. Grzesiek sat on the sofa, silent, looking at the floor. Magda took a deep breath and said:

– You know what? I'm sorry.

Grzesiek looked up.

– For what?

– That I got angry with you. That I didn't tell you I wanted to go out for dinner. I just assumed you knew. And you didn't know. And it's not your fault.

Grzesiek was silent for a moment. Then he said:

– Thank you.

– For what?

– For telling me. That I don't have to guess.

Magda smiled. Maybe this was the first step. Maybe they would finally stop pretending to be perfect. Maybe they would finally start being real.


**Epilogue**

Months passed. Magda and Grzesiek still had their problems – but now they talked about them. It was not always easy, sometimes they fell back into old patterns, but they already knew that expecting each other to guess was a road to nowhere.

Grzesiek began writing down the things Magda told him – so that he would not forget. Magda began saying directly what she needed – without playing guessing games. And although they sometimes still felt lonely in their relationship, they knew that it was only a moment – and that they had someone to turn to.

One evening, when Magda came back from work, Grzesiek stood in the hallway with a smile. He wrapped his arms around her and kissed her on the forehead.

– Was it hard? – he asked.

– Yes – she admitted. – But now it's better.

– I know – he said. – And I'm glad you're here.

Magda hugged him. And for the first time in a long while she felt that she did not have to pretend anything. That she could be herself. And that it was enough.


**THE END**
