## Text

**A TALK WITH THE DOCTOR**

Kasia was thirty-two years old, had two children, an office job and legs that swelled like balloons in the evening. Shoes that were comfortable in the morning started to pinch in the afternoon. At night she was woken by cramps in her calves, and on her ankles blue veins appeared which became more and more visible month after month.

– They are just spider veins – her colleague from work told her. – Every one of us has them.

Kasia believed that for two years. Until one day the first varicose vein appeared on her leg – thick, twisted, blue like a river on a map. And then she stopped believing in "they are just spider veins".

She made an appointment with a vascular surgeon.

---

The office was bright, modern, smelled of disinfectant. On the wall hung a diploma with the title of professor, next to photos from medical conferences. Dr hab. Artur Pupek greeted her with a smile – calm, professional, but not cold.

– Good morning. What brings you to me?

Kasia told him everything. About the swelling, the cramps, the shoes that would not fasten in the evening. About the fact that her mother had varicose veins and her grandmother – ulcers. About the fact that she was afraid the same thing awaited her in twenty years.

The doctor nodded.

– Your mother had varicose veins, your grandmother too. This is a genetic disease. Unfortunately, if venous insufficiency runs in the family, the probability that you will also have it is very high. But it is not a verdict.

Kasia breathed out.

– What should I do?

---

The conversation lasted an hour. Professor Pupek told her everything she should have known, but nobody had told her before.

**About veins and valves**

– Varicose veins do not start in the legs – he began. – They start in the head, in the belief that this is only an aesthetic problem. That you can live with it. Meanwhile, it is a chronic disease which – if untreated – progresses. It begins with swelling and heaviness, then spider veins appear, then varicose veins, and at the end – ulcers. Wounds that do not want to heal. And a smell that cannot be hidden.

Kasia felt a shiver.

– But it does not have to come to that.

– It does not have to – confirmed the doctor. – If we understand how veins work.

The professor took a pen and sketched a simple drawing on a piece of paper.

– Veins are the vessels that carry blood back to the heart. The problem is that this route leads uphill – from the feet to the chest, against gravity. If there were no mechanism, blood would simply fall down. That is why nature equipped our veins with valves – tiny doors that open in only one direction: upwards. When blood flows through, the valve opens. When it tries to flow back, it closes.

– And something destroys them?

– Yes. Veins can widen – for various reasons. Genes, pregnancy, hormones, long periods of standing or sitting. When a vein is too wide, the valve flaps do not meet each other. Blood starts leaking downwards. It is a vicious circle – one collapsed valve overloads the next one, that one also widens, and so on. Eventually varicose veins appear.

– So it is not that I have bad veins – said Kasia. – I have bad valves?

– Exactly. And that changes the perspective, because valves can be offloaded. They can be helped.

---

**The four enemies**

– I distinguish four main enemies of healthy veins – continued the professor. – First, standing still for a long time. When you stand without moving, the calves do not work, and they are the main pump that pushes the blood upwards. You work in an office – how long do you stand?

– Rarely. I sit.

– That is the second enemy. Sitting, especially with one leg crossed over the other. That mechanically compresses the veins around the knee and the groin. Blood stands still. And the third enemy – heat. Hot baths, sauna, heated floors. Heat widens the vessels, and in a person with already weakened valves it is like pouring oil on the fire.

– And the fourth?

– Excess weight. Adipose tissue in the abdominal cavity presses on the inferior vena cava – the main trunk through which blood returns from the legs. Every extra five kilograms increases the pressure in the veins by a few percent. And that pressure works around the clock.

Kasia looked at her legs. She was beginning to understand.

---

**Stockings, medicines, procedures**

– So I should wear compression stockings? – she asked.

– Yes. That is the basis of conservative treatment. Compression stockings squeeze the veins from the outside, reduce their diameter and allow the valves to close properly. Blood flows better, swelling decreases. But – and this is important – stockings do not treat veins. They support them. When you take them off, the veins return to their widened state. It is like with glasses – they improve eyesight, but they do not cure the eyes.

– And medicines?

– Venotonics – diosmin, hesperidin, escin – strengthen the vein walls, reduce the permeability of the vessels. They help, but they also do not repair the valves. They work as long as you take them.

– So how do you get rid of varicose veins?

– The only way to remove a varicose vein is a procedure. And here we have two paths. Surgical – classic vein stripping, anaesthesia, hospital, weeks of recovery. And non-surgical – sclerotherapy.

---

**Sclerotherapy – a modern solution**

– Sclerotherapy involves injecting a special fluid – polidocanol – into the varicose vein in the form of foam. The foam pushes the blood out of the vessel, damages its wall from the inside and makes the vein close. The organism absorbs it within a few weeks. The procedure is minimally invasive, performed in the office, under ultrasound control. It takes about forty minutes. After the procedure the patient stands up, walks for forty minutes and goes home. Without hospital, without general anaesthesia, without weeks of sick leave.

– Does it hurt?

– It should not. You may feel discomfort, a stretching sensation, but it passes. Most importantly, the effect is immediate – varicose veins disappear right away, although for a few weeks hard nodules may be felt in their place. That is normal.

– And complications?

– As with any procedure. Discolouration may occur, which fades after a few months. Rarely – skin ulcers. But the risk is much smaller than with surgery. That is why sclerotherapy is the most willingly chosen method today, especially by active people.

– So I can go back to running?

– The next day. With a compression stocking, but yes. Activity after the procedure is even recommended – walking helps remove the remnants of the fluid from the body.

---

**What to do to prevent varicose veins?**

Kasia took a deep breath.

– And what can I do to prevent them?

– Above all, move. Do not stand still, do not sit with your leg crossed. During office work – get up every hour, walk, do simple exercises: rise on your toes, flex your feet. In the evening – put your legs on a pillow, higher than the heart. A cold shower on the shins contracts the veins. Avoid hot baths. Drink plenty of water. And have your veins examined regularly – Doppler ultrasound once a year, especially if there were varicose veins in the family.

– And if I already have them?

– Treat them. The earlier, the better. You are young, your veins are still in good condition. If we remove the insufficient vessels now and introduce prevention, the chance that the varicose veins will return is smaller.

Kasia was silent for a moment.

– So it is not that I have to come to terms with it?

The professor smiled.

– No. It is not a verdict. It is a chronic disease, but it can be controlled. Like diabetes or hypertension. You just have to know how.

---

**The decision**

Kasia left the office with a piece of paper in her hand – with recommendations, a referral for ultrasound and a phone number to register for the procedure. She was calmer than in the morning.

She sat down on a bench in front of the clinic and looked at her legs. They had varicose veins, but it was not the end of the world. It was the beginning of something else – the awareness that her body is not an enemy, but a system that can be understood. And that doctors – real specialists, not those who promise miracle creams – are on her side.

She took out her phone and wrote to her husband: _"I have signed up for the procedure. No surgery. Tiny needles, an hour, and home. I think I will finally get myself together."_

He replied: _"I have been telling you to do it for ages."_

She smiled. And she felt that in her legs – those aching, swollen, blue ones – lightness appeared. Maybe not physical, but mental. Maybe the first step towards enjoying walking, running, flying again.

---

**Epilogue**

Three months later Kasia came back for a follow-up. There was no trace of varicose veins on her legs. Only light, barely visible discolouration, which was supposed to disappear by summer.

– The effect is excellent – said the professor, looking at the ultrasound. – The veins have closed, the flow is normal. Now only prevention.

– So stockings and movement?

– And one more thing – he added. – Remember that this is not the end. The varicose veins can come back, because the genes stay. But if you take care, many years may pass before they appear. And if they appear – you will know what to do.

Kasia nodded. She left the office and breathed in the spring air. She had a whole summer ahead of her, short skirts and the beach. And legs that did not hurt.

– Thank you, Professor – she said in farewell.

– Thank you – he replied. – For the trust.
