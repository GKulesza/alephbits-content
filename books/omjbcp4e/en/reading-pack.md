## Text

**A MOUNTAIN OUT OF A MOLEHILL**

Adam always lost with Ewa. Not because he was wrong. Not because he lacked arguments. He lost because before he could open his mouth, he was already in a losing position – like a boxer taking blows before the referee even manages to say "fight".

Their first conversation after the separation took place in a café. He chose a public place to limit the risk of scenes. They sat by the window and ordered coffee. He had a black one, she a latte with vanilla syrup – he remembered that she had always drunk it that way, but he did not comment. That was not why they were there.

– I wanted to talk about child support – he began, trying to make his voice sound calm. – I thought we could agree on an amount that would be fair to both sides, without a court.

Ewa set down her cup. Her eyes narrowed slightly, but the smile stayed in place – the same smile that had once disarmed him and now only reminded him how badly he had been mistaken.

– One does not answer a question with a question – she said, though he had not yet asked anything. – But I will ask: why should I be the one to explain myself when you are the one who left?

Adam blinked. He had not asked any question yet, and he was already on the defensive.

– I am not asking you to explain yourself. I am asking about a specific amount that...

– You always put me in this kind of situation – she interrupted him. – As if I were the one at fault. And after all, you are not entirely blameless either, are you?

_You are not entirely blameless._ Adam felt the familiar knot tightening in his stomach. This was one of those conversations. Always the same. He talked about something concrete, she turned it back on him, and he was left with the feeling that he had done something wrong, even though he did not know what.

– I am not saying you are at fault. I am talking about child support.

– Oh, child support. – She sighed theatrically. – What is to hang will not drown. I have always managed somehow, I will manage now too.

– That is not an answer.

– And what can you know about it? – Her voice hardened. – You were not home when I was ill. You were not there when the little one had problems at school. And now you suddenly want to talk about money? You are making a mountain out of a molehill. It is only child support, everyone pays it somehow.

Adam wanted to answer that it was not true – that he had been home, that he remembered the nights by his son's bed, that he was the one who had first taken him to a psychologist. But before he could open his mouth, Ewa was already changing the subject.

– Oh, that is right! We still need to settle the holidays. Your son wants to go to the seaside, and I have no money for it. So instead of sitting here talking about child support, maybe you could think about the child.

And so it ended. Adam left the café without having settled anything. He had lost again.

---
That night he could not sleep. He lay in bed, in his new rented apartment, replaying every exchange in his head. Something was wrong. It was not just that Ewa was angry or hurt – that he understood. The divorce hurt them both. But the way she led the conversation always left him in the same place: confused, guilty, silent.

He reached for his phone. He typed into Google: "how to respond to manipulation in conversation". The algorithm suggested a YouTube video. "10 tricks that make you lose every conversation (and how to change that)". The channel was called Karolina Gorczyca – Barszczewska. He clicked.

An hour later he was sitting on the bed with a notepad in his hand. He had written out every tactic Ewa had used at the café. They were all there.

_One does not answer a question with a question_ – even though he had not asked any question, he had only opened the topic. _You are not entirely blameless_ – turning the accusation around before the accusation had even been made. _What is to hang will not drown_ – a proverb used as a closing argument. _And what can you know about it_ – undermining his right to an opinion. _You are making a mountain out of a molehill_ – downplaying. _Oh, and the holidays_ – a sudden change of subject. Everything delivered at perfect pace, as if Ewa had a ready script in her head.

But the video said something else too: that not every person who uses these tactics does so consciously. Sometimes these are habits brought from home, reflexive defence mechanisms. And that instead of judging intentions, it is better to focus on the reaction. Name what is happening. And return to the topic.

Adam wrote three sentences in his notepad:

_"That is not an answer to my question. I am asking again."_  
_"You may be right, but right now we are talking about this particular matter."_  
_"It matters to me, even if it looks different to you."_

Then he turned off the light and, for the first time in weeks, fell asleep peacefully.

---
Two days later they met again, this time in the presence of a mediator. He was an older man with tired eyes who had already seen hundreds of such couples and had no illusions about human nature. When Adam entered the room, Ewa was already there. She sat at the table with her arms crossed over her chest, ready for a fight.

The mediator opened the meeting with a standard formula about the need for an agreement. Then he gave the floor to Adam.

– I would like us to agree on a specific child support amount. My proposal is twelve hundred zloty a month, plus half of the expenses for school and medical care.

Ewa raised an eyebrow.

– Twelve hundred? You must be joking. And what exactly do you spend so much on? On your new apartment? On all those dates you go on now, while I sit at home with the child?

_You are not entirely blameless._ Adam recognized it immediately. For a moment he felt the old mechanism starting up – the need to explain himself, to defend himself, to prove that he was not what she took him for. But this time he did something different.

– I understand that you have your feelings about my personal life – he said calmly. – But right now we are talking about child support. We can go back to that topic if you want, but first let us finish this one.

Ewa blinked. The mediator made a note in his notebook.

– Fine – she said after a moment, and her voice sounded less sure. – But twelve hundred is too little. After all, you earn twice as much as I do. A wise man is wise after the harm done; now everyone wants to save on their family.

_A proverb as an argument._ Adam almost smiled.

– That is a nice saying, but it does not answer my proposal. If you think twelve hundred is too little, give a specific amount that you consider fair. And justify it.

The mediator nodded. Ewa hesitated. Adam could see her fingers clenching the sleeve of her sweater more tightly.

– Well... I do not know – she said. – Maybe two thousand. But that is not really the point. The point is that you always...

– Always? – Adam raised his hand. – Are we talking about something that happened many times, or about specific situations? Because if it is about specific ones, let us talk about them one by one. If it is about a general impression, we can name it, but let us not substitute one for the other.

Silence fell. Ewa looked at him differently – not at an opponent who could be disarmed with one well-aimed phrase, but at someone who had suddenly stopped playing by her rules.

– You have changed – she said quietly.

– No. I simply learned to stay on topic.

The mediator cleared his throat and returned to the question of child support. The conversation moved on – it was not easy, it was not pleasant, but for the first time in months it was a conversation, not a battle. When Adam left the room, he did not feel triumph. He felt that he had regained something he had lost long ago.

The right to his own opinion.

---
In the evening he sat on the balcony of his new apartment. The air was warm and smelled of linden trees. He opened the notepad at the page where he had written the quote from the video: _"The more you stay calm, the more consistently you stick to the original topic, the less effective each of these tactics becomes."_

He had no illusions. Ewa had not stopped using those tricks. Maybe she never would. But he no longer had to be their victim. It was enough to name the mechanism – and it stopped working on him.

He reached for his phone and wrote her a message. Short, concrete, without emotion.

"We agreed on fifteen hundred plus half of the expenses for school and medical care. I will send the transfer by Friday."

The reply came after a few minutes.

"Fine."

That was all. But Adam knew that behind that single word there was something that had not been there in recent years. Respect. Not for him – not yet. But for the fact that he had finally stood on his own two feet and refused to be thrown off track.

He put the phone in his pocket and looked at the sky. The first star was already showing. He thought that tomorrow he would watch another video on that channel. Maybe the one about gaslighting. And that this was not the end of learning.

It was only the beginning.

**THE END**
