## Text

**THIS YEAR'S GOAL IS... NOTHING**


When Ania entered the office at 7:45, coffee was already waiting on her desk. Not because someone had made it – she had made it herself at home, in a thermal mug she had bought last week so as not to drink that awful coffee from the machine. The thermal mug was part of her New Year's resolutions. Along with the gym, drinking two litres of water every day, and the promise that she would finally write that book she had been thinking about for five years.

It was 7 February. She had been to the gym twice, she had drunk the water for three days, and the book had a title and nothing else.

Ania was thirty-eight, held a director's position at an international corporation, had a husband who loved her more than himself, two children and a house near Warsaw with a garden in which nothing grew, because she had no time to tend it.

From the outside: fulfilled. From her own perspective: empty.

She sat down at her desk, opened her laptop and stared at the empty screen for a moment. Ahead of her was a day that looked exactly like the previous one and like the next one. Meetings, emails, decisions she made on behalf of others, because others didn't have the courage. At the end of the day she would go home, pretend she had energy, put the children to bed, and then, once her husband had fallen asleep, she would scroll through Instagram and wonder how it was possible that others lived life to the full, while she merely existed.

In her head she heard the voice of Karolina, her mentor, whom she had met at their first session a week earlier.

_"What would you do if you knew it would work out?"_

Ania closed her eyes and tried to answer. Her thoughts were like fish in murky water – elusive, quick, vanishing before she could name them.

She opened the drawer and took out a sheet of paper. At the top, in large letters, she wrote: _"GOALS 2026"_.

Below: _"1. Get promoted to the position of board member."_

The pen hung in the air. That was obvious, right? Everyone expected her to strive for promotion. Her boss, her husband, her mother-in-law, who said at every opportunity: "Ania, such a capable girl, so why aren't you CEO yet?"

_"2. Earn 30% more."_

Also obvious. Money gives security, gives opportunities, gives... what? Ania didn't know. She already had enough not to worry about the bills. More money only meant more expenses that brought her no joy.

_"3. Lose 8 kilograms."_

At this one she even smiled. Lose weight, so that she could finally like herself. But were the kilograms the problem? Wasn't it the case that she didn't like herself for completely different reasons, and her weight was only a convenient explanation?

_"4. Write a book."_

That was her goal. Her real goal. Only she didn't have the courage. She was afraid that something silly would come out of it, that someone would read it and say: "And all those years you thought you had something to say?"

She put down the pen. The sheet with the goals looked like the wish list of someone she didn't know. As if it had been written by another woman, one who believed that fulfilment is the sum of achievements.

---


## II

The second session with Karolina took place over Zoom. Ania sat in the study in which she never rested, because it was meant for work.

– Does any of these things really make you happy? – asked Karolina.

Ania looked at the list.

– The promotion? I don't know. I think it means I'm good.

– And the book?

Ania hesitated.

– The book... that's something I've always wanted to do. But I'm afraid it will be a waste of time.

– And what would happen if you wrote it and nobody bought it?

– I'd be sad.

– And what would happen if you never wrote it at all?

Ania was silent.

– If I never wrote it, then... I'd never know. But in fact I feel the same way now. I don't know.

Karolina smiled.

– Look, Ania. That goal, the book, is yours. The rest – the promotion, the money, the weight – those are goals someone else set. Or that you set for yourself because you think you should have them. And a real goal is the one that drives you, the one that makes you feel joy, even when it's hard. When you think about writing, what do you feel?

– Strange. Fear, but... an exciting kind.

– That is exactly your goal. The rest is just decoration.

Ania looked at the list. She took the pen and crossed out the first three items.

– What's left is the book – she said.

– What's left – agreed Karolina. – And now the question: what's blocking you? Why haven't you written it in five years?

Ania closed her eyes.

– I'm afraid I can't do it. That I have no talent. That everyone will laugh at me.

– Where does that belief come from?

– I don't know. Maybe from childhood. My mother always said it's best not to stick out, so you don't fall. And that once you do something, you either do it perfectly or not at all.

Karolina nodded.

– And how do you feel about that belief?

– As if something is holding my legs while I try to run.

– And what if you put that belief aside for a moment? What if you thought: "It's not true that I have to be perfect. I can simply be good enough." What would happen?

Ania opened her eyes.

– I think... I'd start writing.

---


## III

Weeks passed. Ania started getting up an hour earlier to write. The first days were a nightmare – she felt she was creating something worthless, that she was wasting time. But Karolina told her about the 15-minute method: _"Sit down for 15 minutes and just write. Without judging, without correcting. Then you can stop."_

15 minutes turned into 30, then into an hour. After three weeks Ania had 50 pages. She didn't know whether they were good, but she was proud of herself. And that was something she hadn't felt in years – pride in herself.

In the meantime her life began to change. She hadn't planned it, but she stopped caring what others thought. When her boss offered her a promotion, she thanked him and declined. When her mother-in-law started talking about a "career", she smiled and said: "I have other plans."

The weight dropped on its own, without a diet. She started drinking water because she felt like it. She stopped eating sweets because she didn't need them. And then she understood something Karolina had said at one of the sessions:

_"A goal is not only what you want to achieve. A goal is who you want to become."_

Ania wanted to become a woman who isn't afraid to dream. Who doesn't have to prove anything to anyone. Who simply is.

---


## IV

One evening, when her husband Piotr came back from work, he found her in the living room, on the sofa, with a laptop on her knees. She was wearing soft pyjamas, her hair was messy, and on her face was a smile he hadn't seen in years.

– You're writing? – he asked, sitting down next to her.

– I'm writing – she answered.

– And how's it going?

– Well. Very well, even.

Piotr looked at the screen. He saw a chapter in which the heroine – clearly modelled on Ania – was making the decision to give everything up and start living her own way.

– Is this about you? – he asked.

– A little. But also not. It's about a woman who discovers that you can be happy even if you don't have all the answers.

Piotr took her hand.

– And do you have the answers?

– No. But I know I've stopped looking for them outside myself. And that's the greatest goal I've ever achieved.

---


## V

Eight months later, in November, Ania got an email from the publisher. Her book had been accepted. It was going to be published in the spring.

She didn't scream. She didn't jump. She just sat down and looked out of the window for a long time. Outside it was raining, the same rain as on the day she had started writing. Only she was different.

She picked up the sheet of paper on which she had written her goals a year earlier. She crossed out the last item and wrote at the bottom:

_"5. Be myself. Done."_

And then she understood that the most important goal a person can set is the one that cannot be written down. The goal that has no completion date, because it lasts a lifetime. The goal that is called: _to live in harmony with oneself_.

Once she had thought that goals are dreams with a date. Now she knew that was only part of the truth. Because the most important dreams don't have dates – they only have the courage to reach for them.

And the courage to sometimes give them up, when they turn out not to be ours.

She closed the laptop. She got up and went to the window. The rain was still falling, but the sky above the horizon was beginning to clear.

_"What would you do if you knew it would work out?"_ – she heard in her head.

She smiled.

– I'd do what I'm doing. Only faster.

---


## VI

A year later Ania met Karolina for coffee. Her book had sold a few thousand copies, and she herself gave talks about how to find your goal without losing yourself.

– And what now? – asked Karolina. – Do you have a new goal?

Ania thought for a moment.

– I do. I want to learn to be grateful for what I have, instead of constantly chasing what I don't have. I want to be able to stop.

– That sounds like a lifelong goal.

– Because it is a lifelong goal.

Karolina smiled.

– You know that's the hardest goal of all?

– I know. That's why I chose it.

And then they both laughed. Because after all – what if each of us had such a goal? To be enough. Here and now.

---



**THE END**
