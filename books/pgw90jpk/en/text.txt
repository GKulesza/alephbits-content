**THE FIRST DAY OF THE REST OF YOUR LIFE**

Marta woke up first, as always. She lay still for a moment, staring at the ceiling and listening to Darek's breathing – even, calm, as if nothing bad had been happening between them for months. Or maybe for years. Grey November light seeped through the gap in the curtains. Rain was falling outside the window.

She didn't feel like getting up. Not because she was tired – she had slept eight hours – but because the day that was about to begin was exactly the same as the one before. And the one before that. And the one before that. Coffee, breakfast, silence at the table, then she to work, he to work, in the evening a series that they both stared at, but neither of them could remember what the episode had been about afterwards. And so on, in circles. Harmony, disharmony, only without any repair.

In the kitchen she set up the coffee machine and took the eggs out of the fridge. Darek came downstairs a few minutes later – in sweatpants, hair dishevelled, phone in hand. He didn't even look her way.

– You didn't put the cap back on the toothpaste again – she said, without turning away from the stove.

Silence. Then a sigh.

– Stop it, Marta. You're nagging again.

– I'm not nagging. I'm just telling you.

– You're always telling me something. – He sat down at the table without looking up from his phone. – Toothpaste, dishes, rubbish. If it's not one thing, it's another. Nitpicking.

Marta clenched her fingers on the spatula. _Nitpicking._ She knew that word. She had heard it hundreds of times in different versions. _You're overreacting. You're making it up again. Give it a rest._ Every time she felt something inside her go out – that little spark of hope that maybe this time he would hear her. That maybe this time he would say: _You're right, I'm sorry, let's talk._ But he never said that.

– It's not about the toothpaste – she said quietly.

– Then what is it about? – He raised his head. There was no anger in his eyes. There was something worse – tiredness. Not with her, but with the whole situation. With them.

– It's about the fact that you don't even try.

– Try what?

– Us.

He put the phone down on the table, screen down. That gesture – done slowly, almost theatrically – meant that now he was giving her his full attention, but not because he was listening. Because she was making complaints. Again.

– Marta, what do you want? – he asked in a tone she knew perfectly well. The same one he used when his boss dumped an extra project on him on a Friday at four in the afternoon. Tired, condescending, barely masking irritation.

– Nothing. – She turned to the stove. Scrambled eggs were sizzling in the pan. – Nothing anymore.

And then, at exactly that moment, she remembered the podcast she had listened to in the car yesterday. Two women were talking about relationships at a crossroads. One of them – Natalia, an author – said something that stuck in Marta's head. _The "instead of complaining, let's take responsibility" method._ It sounded like a spell. Or like a sentence.

She turned off the gas. She turned to Darek.

– I want to tell you something.

– Go on. – He shrugged.

– When I tell you that something bothers me – like the toothpaste, the dishes, anything – and you answer "stop it, you're nagging again", I feel bad. Very bad. And it's not about the fact that you don't put the cap back on the toothpaste. It's about the fact that every time I talk about what hurts me, you belittle it. You say I'm overreacting. That I'm making it up. And I don't want to talk about it anymore. I don't want to complain, I don't want to torment you. But I want you to know one thing.

She hesitated. Her heart was pounding so hard she could feel it in her temples. Darek was looking at her – for the first time in a long time with something that resembled alertness.

– What? – he asked.

– I can't go on functioning like this. When you react to what I say that way. And I'll stop complaining about it. I won't remind you that your "stop it" hurts me. But what you do with that information – that will be the sign for me. Whether I still want to be here.

Silence fell. The kind they had never had before. Not angry, not sulky. Just heavy with meaning.

Darek said nothing for a moment. His face went through several stages – surprise, irritation, something like uncertainty. Then he stood up.

– I'm going to get dressed – he said.

And he left.

Marta stood by the stove, staring at the door that closed behind him with a quiet click. The scrambled eggs had gone cold. She felt cold too – but not in a bad way. Not the way she felt when the spark went out inside her. Rather like someone who had finally stopped banging her head against a wall.

She didn't know what Darek would do. Maybe in an hour he would come back, maybe he would say "I'm sorry, I didn't know it mattered that much to you." Or maybe in a week he would throw out "stop it, you're nagging again" and then she would have to do what she had announced.

But for the first time in a very long time, she didn't feel powerless. Because she had done something differently than usual. She hadn't started another row. She hadn't said "we need to talk" in a tone that immediately put him on the defensive. She had simply set a boundary. And given him a choice.

She walked over to the table. His phone was still lying there, screen down. Next to it – an empty coffee cup, crumbs from last night's dinner, a napkin crumpled into a ball. She didn't clean up. She left it as it was.

She took her cup, sat down by the window and watched the rain lash against the pane. Somewhere in the house a door creaked – Darek was moving around upstairs. In a moment he would come down. Maybe he would say he was sorry. Maybe he would say she was dramatizing. Or maybe he wouldn't say anything, just take his keys and leave for work, as always.

Whatever he did, she already knew. _Today begins the next day of the rest of her life._ And it was up to her how she would live it.

**THE END**
