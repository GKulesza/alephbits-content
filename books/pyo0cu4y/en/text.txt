**FIREHOPPER**

In the old manor house that stood at the edge of a great forest, time flowed differently than anywhere else. The walls remembered the days when the great-great-grandparents of the current owners still lived under their roof, and in the chimneys the wind played melodies that no one could name anymore. The manor was beautiful and mysterious, full of nooks, old books and places no one had looked into for years.

Three siblings lived in that manor — Zosia, Franek and little Antek. Their parents had gone on a long journey, leaving the children in the care of Mr Stanisław, a young teacher who was also a poet and a dreamer. And it was just then, when the parents had left, that strange things began to happen in the manor.

---

On the first night, when everyone was already asleep, Franek suddenly woke up. He heard a quiet knocking coming from behind the wall. He got up, walked to the door and opened it carefully. The corridor was dark, but from the attic came a soft, sneaking rustle.

Franek knew he shouldn't go there. But curiosity was stronger. He took a torch and climbed up the creaking stairs.

In the attic it was cold and smelled of old wood. Suddenly he heard singing — quiet, as if coming from nowhere:

_Firehopper, firehopper, he jumps around the flues, in the old manor plays his tricks, and on his head a little red cap!_

Franek jumped. Before him, on the edge of the great chimney, stood a tiny little man dressed in red robes. On his head he wore a little red cap, and in his hand he held a little stick with which he traced fiery circles in the air.

– Who are you? – asked Franek in a trembling voice.

The little man laughed mockingly.

– I am Firehopper! I've lived in this manor for ages, but nobody ever saw me, because I was only a little spark. And now, thanks to your pranks and your anger, I've grown! I'm going to rule here!

Franek wanted to run, but his legs were tangled. Firehopper hopped up and waved his stick — at the same moment the door slammed behind Franek and a shower of sparks burst from the chimney.

The boy raced down the stairs, and behind him all that could be heard was the goblin's laughter.

---

From that day on, real havoc began in the manor. Chairs moved across the rooms by themselves, books fell from the shelves, and in the kitchen Katarzyna's slippers, the older servant, came to life and chased her all over the house. Firehopper was having a wonderful time.

The worst was when the goblin decided to take up teaching. Mr Stanisław was just holding a lesson for the children when suddenly the inkwell tipped over by itself and a book fell from the highest shelf. Firehopper loved to interfere.

– We have to do something – said Mr Stanisław, brushing the ink off his clothes. – This goblin is getting bolder. If we don't find a way to stop him, the manor will never know peace.

– Maybe there's a book about spirits in the library? – suggested Zosia.

And they were right. In the oldest part of the library, behind a pile of dusty volumes, Mr Stanisław found a book bound in old leather. It bore the title: _"Of spirits, witchcraft and spells — the work of Master Adalbert"_.

When he opened the book, he found a chapter about Firehopper. He read it carefully.

– Here's the way – he said at last. – Firehopper is greedy and can't resist sweets. We have to prepare a trap for him. But first we need his little red cap, because that's what gives him his power.

---

The next day the children and Mr Stanisław prepared the bait: a big glass jar filled with honey and sweet preserves. They placed it in the chamber where the goblin spent most of his time, and began reading the spells from Master Adalbert's book.

At first Firehopper wouldn't be fooled. He circled the jar, sniffing suspiciously, but in the end the smell of sweets proved stronger. He jumped onto the rim of the jar, slid inside and started eating.

Then Mr Stanisław knocked on the book three times and called out in a booming voice:

– Abracadabra! Let the will of Master Adalbert be done!

Zosia and Franek immediately stopped the mouth of the jar with a cork, and Antek sealed it with wax and pressed a cross into it.

Firehopper was trapped!

---

The goblin flew into a rage. He thrashed around in the jar, shouted, threatened.

– Set me free, and I'll give you gold, silver, diamonds! I have a whole underground kingdom!

– We don't believe you – Mr Stanisław said calmly. – But you will show us the way to your kingdom. If you behave, perhaps we'll set you free.

Firehopper ground his teeth, but he had to agree. He showed them the way to the old chimney, where, after turning two bricks, an entrance to the underground appeared.

They climbed down an old, slippery ladder. The corridors were dark and damp, and the walls were covered with stalactites. Suddenly they came upon a huge lake of crystal-clear water, and on its shore — the skeleton of a giant ichthyosaur.

– Look – whispered Mr Stanisław. – This is evidence of ancient times, when monsters like these swam here at the bottom of the ocean.

They went further, until they reached a chamber full of gold and jewels. The walls glittered, and a metallic smell hung in the air.

– So much treasure! – cried Antek.

– That's just Firehopper's loot – said Mr Stanisław. – We can't take it, because he's its guardian. Our goal is the cap.

And then, just as they were about to take it off the goblin's head, Franek tripped and let go of the jar. The glass jar smashed against the stones, and Firehopper leapt out of it like a spark, bursting into mocking laughter.

– Fools! – he laughed. – Did you think you could beat me that easily? Now you'll taste my revenge!

He began throwing stones at them, raising clouds of sand, and the waves of the lake crashed against the shore with a roar. The children screamed in fear.

But Mr Stanisław didn't lose hope. He opened Master Adalbert's book and read:

_"When the jar breaks and the goblin escapes, do not fear. His power lies in the little red cap. When you tear it from his head, he will lose all his strength."_

Zosia was holding in her hand the little red cap that had fallen out of the jar the moment it broke.

– Mr Stanisław! – she cried. – I've got it!

– Throw it to me! – shouted the teacher.

Zosia threw the cap, and Mr Stanisław caught it and cried:

– In the name of Master Adalbert, so be it! Abracadabra!

Firehopper began to shrink, to twist, and in the end — he turned into a small red pebble that rolled to Mr Stanisław's feet.

---

Silence fell. The children breathed a sigh of relief.

– We did it! – cried Antek.

– Yes – confirmed Mr Stanisław. – But remember: Firehopper was born of your pranks and bad thoughts. If you start quarrelling again, playing silly tricks and being unkind to each other, a new goblin may be born. And then it may be even harder to defeat him.

The children nodded. On the way back they decided to be better to one another.

When they returned to the manor, dawn was already breaking beyond the windows. Katarzyna, who hadn't slept all night, hugged the children tearfully. Mr Stanisław told her briefly what had happened, but told her to keep it a secret.

– Let this stay between us – he said. – And let this story be a warning for us all.

From that day on, peace reigned in the manor. The children tried to be good, not to quarrel and to help one another. And whenever one of them felt like playing a prank, they remembered Firehopper and gave up.

The little red pebble was never found again. And Master Adalbert's old book returned to its place in the library, where it waited for the next daredevil brave enough to learn its secrets.

**THE END**
