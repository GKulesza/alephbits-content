**THE BREAK**

## I. 22:47

When Patryk closed his laptop, his eyes were burning. He had worked fourteen hours. The presentation he was to give tomorrow morning was ready, but he felt no satisfaction. He felt only emptiness. And that tremor inside, which had been with him for weeks.

He got up from the desk, walked over to the window and looked at the city. Warsaw at night looked like an ocean of lights – beautiful, distant, indifferent. He took a deep breath, but the air stuck somewhere in his throat. He could not breathe. He did not remember when he had last truly breathed.

He returned to the desk and opened a drawer. Inside lay an old notebook his wife had given him for his birthday three years ago. He had never used it. He always had too much on his mind to write down thoughts. Too much to think.

For a moment he looked at the blank first page. Then, not knowing why, he reached for a pen and wrote:

_"I do not remember when I last felt truly well. I do not remember when I last felt that I was breathing. Everything is too fast, too loud, too much."_

He put down the pen and closed the notebook. He did not know why he had written that. Maybe because in the middle of the night, when no one is watching, it is easier to admit what you feel.

He lay down on the bed, but he could not fall asleep. Thoughts circled endlessly, like a record stuck in one place. Tomorrow's presentation. The boss who is never satisfied. Another project, another deadline, another day in which he would pretend he was coping.

He fell asleep towards morning, but sleep brought no relief. He woke up tired, with a headache and the feeling that something inside him was about to break.

---

## II. 07:34

The coffee was hot, but he did not feel it. He drank it automatically, as every day, without taste, without pleasure. He got into the car and turned on the radio. Someone was talking about politics, someone else about the weather. He turned it off. He put on music, but that did not help either. Everything was just noise.

In the traffic jam on Wisłostrada he clenched his hands on the steering wheel. In front of him brake lights flashed; behind him someone honked. He felt the tension rising in him like a wave about to flood the whole shore. He knew there was no point in getting angry – it would not speed up the traffic, would not change anything. And yet he felt that anger, that helplessness spreading over him like hot lead.

And then, for the first time in weeks, something came to his mind. He remembered the words he had heard in some podcast a week ago, as he was falling asleep:

_"Do I have to be stressed about this?"_

He paused for a moment. He looked at the lights, at the cars, at himself. And he asked himself the question out loud:

— Do I have to be stressed about this?

The answer was obvious: no. He did not have to. It would not change anything. And yet his body was tense, his breath shallow, his heart beating too fast.

He took a deep breath. Then another. Then a third.

He felt silly. Inhale, exhale. What was that supposed to change? But inhale, exhale.

And then he felt something strange. For a fraction of a second, between one breath and the next, something inside him relaxed. Not for long – just for a moment. But it was something.

He remembered what that woman had said then:

_"It is not only about breathing in the moment of stress. It is about taking care of your nervous system every day."_

The nervous system. Until now he had thought of it as something abstract – something that exists but that you do not feel. And now, in the traffic jam, in the car, before the presentation he was afraid of, he felt it physically. The tension in his shoulders. The cramp in his stomach. The feeling that something was about to break.

And he understood that it was not his fault. That his body was doing what it was programmed to do – responding to a threat. Only it could not tell whether it was a real threat or just an email from the boss.

His brain did not know that he was not dying. And that was why he felt as if he were dying.

---

## III. 18:45

The presentation went well. Or badly – Patryk was not sure. The boss nodded, teammates patted him on the shoulder, but he felt no relief. He felt only tiredness. And that strange feeling of being in his body, but not quite.

He came home earlier than usual. His wife looked at him, surprised.

— Did something happen?

— No. I do not think so. I just... need a moment.

He went to the bedroom and lay down on the bed. For a moment he lay in silence, staring at the ceiling. And then he heard something he had not heard in a long time – his own breath. Slow, steady, the way it was when nothing was weighing him down.

He closed his eyes and tried to scan his body, as that woman had said. He started with his feet. They were cold. Then his legs – tense. His belly – drawn in. His chest – tight. His shoulders – raised, as if he were constantly waiting for a blow.

With every breath he tried to relax the next part of his body. It was not going well. Thoughts kept coming back, his heart beat too fast, he felt silly lying there and breathing. But something inside him told him not to stop.

After ten minutes he got up. Nothing great had happened – he had not felt sudden peace, he had had no revelation. But he felt... different. As if someone had taken off him a heavy coat he had been wearing for years without realizing it.

He went to the kitchen, where his wife was making dinner. She came up to him and hugged him.

— Everything all right? – she asked.

— I think so. For the first time in a long while.

— What happened?

— I stopped. And thought.

— About what?

Patryk hesitated. How do you explain to someone something you do not yet fully understand yourself?

— About the fact that... that I do not have to be stressed. That I can choose differently.

His wife looked at him carefully.

— And did you choose?

— I am trying.

She smiled and went back to cooking. Patryk sat at the table and looked at her for a moment. She was there, for years. She was always there. And he was never truly present. He always thought about work, about deadlines, about what still had to be done. He was always somewhere else.

Not today. Today he was here.

---

## IV. 22:47, three weeks later

Patryk sat at the desk, but this time not in front of the laptop. In front of him lay the old notebook in which, three weeks ago, he had written his first sentence. Now he opened it and read:

_"I do not remember when I last felt truly well."_

He smiled. Not because it was funny – because something had changed. Not everything, not at once, not completely. But something.

Over the past three weeks, every day he had found a moment to stop. Sometimes five minutes, sometimes ten. Sometimes only as long as a deep breath in the traffic jam. He noticed when his body tensed up. He noticed when his thoughts started to gallop. And instead of falling into them, he simply breathed.

He did not always succeed. Sometimes he forgot, sometimes he had no strength, sometimes it seemed pointless. But he came back. And every time he came back, he felt he was doing something good.

He took a pen and wrote on a new page:

_"Today I was not stressed. Which means: I was, but I noticed it. And that was enough."_

He closed the notebook and put it on the desk. Next to it stood a lamp casting a soft, yellow light on the pages. Patryk looked at the sight for a moment. At the notebook, at the lamp, at his own hands resting on the desk.

And then he understood something he had not understood before. That what he called stress was not an enemy. It was information. A signal from the body that something was wrong. And that the answer to it was not fighting or fleeing, but stopping. Breathing. Being.

He got up, walked to the bed and lay down next to his wife. He closed his eyes. And for the first time in a very long time, he fell asleep peacefully.
