**THE KEY TO THE RAINY LAND**

Zosia was nine years old and lived in a little house at the edge of a great forest. Her mother was a gardener, and her father worked at the nearby mill. Zosia loved the flowers that grew in her mother's garden, and she loved listening to her grandmother's stories – an elderly lady with silver hair and eyes like two blue lakes.

Grandma always had time for Zosia. She taught her to recognise birds by their song, to name the clouds in the sky and to listen to what the wind whispers. And in the evenings, when the first stars lit up in the sky, she told her stories about enchanted lands, about rain queens and about flowers that sang on moonlit nights.

One summer a great drought fell upon the fields. The sun blazed mercilessly, the earth cracked, and the flowers in mother's garden began to wither. Father came back from the mill worried, saying that there was less and less water in the stream.

Zosia was very worried. She walked sadly through the garden, stroked the withered leaves and tried to water them, but the bucket of water vanished into the cracked earth in the blink of an eye.

One afternoon, as the sun began to hide behind the forest, her grandmother called her over.

– Zosia, come here for a moment – she said in a mysterious voice.

The girl ran to her grandmother, who was sitting in her favourite armchair by the window. On her lap she held an old, leather-bound book.

– Grandma, what book is that? – asked Zosia, sitting down on the rug at her feet.

– This is not an ordinary book – answered her grandmother with a smile. – This is a key to another world.

She opened the book to the first page, and Zosia saw a beautifully painted map of a land full of forests, mountains and rivers. In the very middle of the map stood a castle that shone like a pearl in the moonlight.

– Do you see that castle? – asked her grandmother.

– Yes, grandma. How beautiful it is!

– That is the castle of the Rain Queen. Her name is Kropeia. If we could find her, we would ask her for rain for our land.

– And how can we find her? – asked Zosia with hope in her voice.

Grandma closed the book and took a small, silver key out of her apron pocket.

– This key opens the door to the enchanted path. But the journey will not be easy. You must pass through three lands and guess the riddles you meet along the way. Remember, Zosia – listen with your heart, and you will find the answers.

– I'll go, grandma! I must help!

Grandma smiled and nodded. She stood up, walked over to the old door in the corner of the room, which had always been locked, and put the key in the lock. The door opened, and before Zosia appeared a path lined with flowers, leading deep into the enchanted forest.

– Good luck, my dear – whispered her grandmother. – And remember that magic lives in you too.

Zosia took a deep breath and stepped onto the path.

---
The first land was all golden and orange. Instead of trees, huge mushrooms grew there, and the grass was soft as velvet. At the end of the path stood a house that looked like a giant pumpkin. In front of the house stood a guard – a small, green creature with antennae and big eyes.

– Welcome, traveller! – called the creature. – If you want to pass through my land, you must sing me a song. Only that song will open the door of the pumpkin house.

Zosia remembered the words her grandmother had taught her, and she sang:

_Drip, drip, drip, please open the door, to the Rain Queen I'll be closer than before._

The door of the house opened with a soft creak, and Zosia went inside.

Inside it was warm and cosy. On the shelves stood jars full of honey, and on the table lay fragrant bread and cheese. In the corner of the room stood an elderly lady in glasses, stirring something in a big pot.

– Good day, my child! – she said. – I can smell the forest on you. Are you ready for a riddle?

Zosia nodded.

The elderly lady blindfolded her with a scarf and handed her three fruits. Zosia touched them, smelled them and tasted them. The first was sweet and fragrant like holidays – it was a banana. The second was heavy, firm and juicy – a watermelon. The third was small, round and sweet – a grape.

– Bravo, Zosia! – called the elderly lady. – You are cleverer than you think.

She gave Zosia a piece of fragrant apple pie for the road and pointed to the door leading to the second land.

---
The second land was green and wild. The trees were so dense that light barely broke through them. Colourful parrots sat on the branches, and small, fluffy rabbits ran among the bushes. At the end of the path stood a little house covered in wild ivy, and before it kept watch a guard – a big, gentle dog.

Zosia sang the song, the door opened, and inside a strange, talking butterfly was waiting for her. It sat down on a golden armchair and said:

– Welcome, Zosia. I have three sound riddles for you. Are you ready?

Zosia nodded.

The first sound: _cock-a-doodle-doo!_

– A rooster! – cried Zosia.

The second sound: _moo!_

– A cow!

The third sound: _chirp-chirp-chirp!_

Zosia smiled.

– Crickets. In summer we listen to them in the garden with grandma.

The butterfly clapped its wings.

– You are very observant! – it said. – Here is your reward.

It gave her a warm, white cloak with a hood and a necklace with the words "Friends forever".

– Give this to your grandmother – it whispered. – Tell her that an old friend still remembers her.

---
The third land was white and cold. Snow was falling, and the air was filled with the sounds of magical music. The house at the end of the road was made entirely of snow and ice, and the guard was a tall drum, which danced when Zosia sang.

Inside reigned a magical chill, but the snow was warm to the touch. By a harp sat a beautiful woman with long, silver hair.

– Welcome, Zosia – she said. – Your grandmother has told me about you. You have two tasks ahead of you.

The first task: repeat the rhythm. Zosia clapped her hands when the woman played the harp. She did it perfectly.

The second task: play on a silver flute the melody that carries warmth. Zosia concentrated and played, and the notes spread through the whole house, melting the ice.

– You are ready to meet the Queen – said the woman. – But remember – Kropeia's gown is frozen. You must thaw it with your melody.

---
Zosia sailed in a boat across a great lake to the rainy castle. Inside she found Queen Kropeia, standing still, bound by an icy gown.

– Help me! – pleaded the Queen.

Zosia took out the flute and played the melody of warmth. The gown melted, and Kropeia smiled.

– Thank you, brave girl! – she cried. – Now I will help you.

They jumped onto a cloud and flew over the dried-up land. Kropeia sang the song of rain, and a heavy rain poured down from the clouds. The earth came back to life, the flowers raised their heads, and new buds bloomed in mother's garden.

---
When Zosia returned home, her grandmother was waiting for her with open arms.

– You did it, my dear! – she said, hugging her granddaughter.

Zosia handed her grandmother the necklace and the flute, told her about the butterfly and about the beautiful woman with the harp. Grandmother laughed quietly.

– You see, Zosia – she said. – The world is full of magic, if only we know how to see it. And you, my dear, have the greatest magic in you – a good heart.

From that day on, Zosia knew that a hero hides in each of us. Sometimes it is enough to open a door that seems locked and to trust your own heart. Because true magic is not in spells – it is in love, in caring for others and in the courage to set out into the unknown.

And in the evening, before sleep, as Zosia looked at the starry sky, she heard her grandmother's quiet whisper:

_Drip, drip, drip... Please open the door, to the Rain Queen I'll be closer than before..._

And she knew that whenever she wished, the enchanted lands would open before her again.

**THE END**
