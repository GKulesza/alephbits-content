**THE END OF THE BOX ERA**

When Adam first read the news about Sony's decision, he thought it was an early April Fools' joke. It was July 8, 2026, and he was sitting in his apartment in Warsaw, sipping coffee and scrolling through social media. Suddenly his eyes landed on a headline that made him stop breathing for a few seconds.

_"Sony is ending production of discs for new PlayStation games starting January 2028."_

He put down his phone. He took a deep breath. Then he took it again.

– That's impossible – he said to himself.

Adam had been a gamer since childhood. He remembered the days when, as a ten-year-old, he got his first PlayStation under the Christmas tree. He remembered the smell of a new disc as he took it out of the box. He remembered the joy of collecting, of arranging the boxes on a shelf, of looking at them and knowing they were _his_. That no one could take them away.

And now Sony was saying that all of this was supposed to end.

**First reaction**

Adam immediately went on Twitter. He knew he wasn't alone. And he was right – the platform exploded. The hashtags #SaveTheDisc, #PlayStationIsOver and #PhysicalMediaMatters were trending. People posted photos of their disc collections, screenshots of cancelled PlayStation Plus subscriptions, memes about Sony as the "new Ubisoft".

But what struck him most was something else. One of the first community notes that appeared under Sony's official announcement reminded players of something many of them had been forgetting:

_"Digital purchases are not ownership. They are a revocable licence to access content."_

Adam felt a chill. He remembered the scandal from a few months earlier, when Sony removed hundreds of purchased Studio Canal films from users' libraries over licensing issues. People had paid for content and then – suddenly – no longer had it.

– The same thing could happen to games – he thought.

And then he started to act.

**Cancelling the subscription**

Adam had had PlayStation Plus for years. He couldn't even remember when he last played without it. It was such a habit – you paid every month, got free games, played online, and sometimes forgot to cancel.

Until now.

He went into his account settings. He found the "Subscriptions" tab. He clicked "Cancel". The system asked: "Are you sure you want to give up PlayStation Plus? You will lose access to the free games and the multiplayer mode."

Adam clicked "Yes".

He felt relief. But he knew this was only the beginning.

**A community in action**

That same evening Adam joined several groups on Facebook and Discord where players were organising a boycott. People shared screenshots of cancelled subscriptions, urged others to do the same, and discussed what further steps to take.

Someone posted a link to a petition. Someone else published instructions on how to file a complaint with Sony. Yet another person reminded everyone of the growing interest in jailbreaking the PS5 – illegal, but for many the only way to keep control over their own hardware.

Adam read everything. He saw how people who, just a few days earlier, had been Sony fans were now turning against the company. He saw anger, disappointment, but also determination.

– Maybe something will change after all – he thought.

**A voice from above**

A few days later Adam came across a video in which the former president of Blizzard, Mike Ybarra, commented on the situation. Ybarra said that the move towards digital distribution had probably been only a matter of time, but stressed that platforms must give players something like a _digital promise_:

– Players should have a clear guarantee that purchased games will still work in the future. We need a digital library that would give certainty of keeping access to purchases, make sharing games easier, and even allow a market for reselling digital copies.

Adam nodded at the screen.

– That's exactly it – he said.

**Politicians enter the game**

Then the matter gained even more attention. The French politician and presidential candidate Jean-Luc Mélenchon linked Sony's decision to GTA 6 and reminded everyone that in a fully digital model the player pays but is not necessarily the owner. He cannot lend the game, cannot resell it, and has no full guarantee of keeping it.

Mélenchon announced that in 2027 he wanted to launch a legislative initiative on the matter.

Adam read this and felt that what had begun as a fan war over boxes was now turning into something far more serious. Into a dispute over consumer rights, digital ownership, and protecting games as part of culture.

**Sony's silence**

Meanwhile, Sony was silent. For five days after the announcement, PlayStation's main channels published no new posts. No memes, no trailers, no interaction with fans.

Adam watched this with a mixture of amusement and frustration.

– Like rats – he muttered, quoting the words of one YouTuber. – They've hidden in their holes.

He knew the company had probably counted on the wave of anger passing. That people would forget once a new trailer appeared. But Adam also knew that in the digital world, memory lasts longer than it used to.

**The hours counter and regional blocks**

In the meantime, more problems appeared. Some PlayStation 5 users reported that their counter of hours played in physical games had disappeared. Sony explained it as a bug, but the timing was absolutely terrible.

– In the very week the company tells players that new physical releases are to become a thing of the past, a bug appears that concerns exactly the games on discs – Adam said to his friend who played on PC. – It's as if they shot themselves in the foot.

On top of that came the regional block of Marvel's Tokon on Steam – the game was unavailable in 132 countries that had no access to PlayStation Network. It recalled the Helldivers 2 affair, when the PSN account requirement suddenly made a game unavailable to users in regions without official access to the service.

– This isn't only about discs – Adam thought. – This is about Sony's entire business model. About how they want to lock us into their ecosystem and control every aspect of our gaming.

**The decision**

A week later Adam made a decision. He went to a forum where people were discussing the future of PlayStation. He wrote a post that went viral:

_"I have a PS5. I have hundreds of games on discs. I've had a PS Plus subscription for years. But if Sony goes through with this plan – the PS6 will be the first console I won't buy. Not because I don't like games. Because I don't like being treated like a customer who has no rights to what he buys."_

The post drew hundreds of comments. People agreed, shared their own stories, urged each other to act.

Adam closed his laptop. He looked at his shelf of games. Hundreds of boxes. Each one had its own story. Each was proof that once – in another world – what you bought was yours forever.

– Maybe this will change – he thought. – Maybe if we shout loudly enough, they will hear us. And if not... well, there's always the PC.

He smiled bitterly. He knew this was not the end. That it was only the beginning of a long struggle for a digital future in which players would not only be consumers, but also owners.

And he was ready to take part in that struggle.

**THE END**
