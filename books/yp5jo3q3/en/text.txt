**RETURN TO ONESELF**

## I. THE BEGINNING

Professor Marek Kowalski woke up at 5:47, as usual. He lay in the darkness of the bedroom, hearing his wife's steady breathing beside him. A stack of documents waited for him on the desk – materials for another article on the neurobiology of consciousness, which he had been working on for months.

He was a materialist. A physicist. A man who believed that everything could be explained by equations, measurements and hard data. Consciousness? Just an emergent property of the brain. Spirituality? An escape from reality. God? A concept science had buried long ago.

He got up, drank some coffee and sat down at his desk. He opened his laptop. A message from the editor flashed on the screen: _"Marek, the deadline is in a week. How's it going?"_

The progress was nonexistent. Not because he had nothing to write. He had plenty. But for a week something had been blocking him. Something he couldn't name.

He closed his eyes. And then he saw it.

Not a dream, not an image, but _something in between_. As if someone had put into his head a memory he didn't have. He saw himself – but not as he was now. He saw light. Dense, golden light that flowed through him like water. And he heard a voice – not words, but a feeling: _"You are part of the whole. You are not a body."_

He opened his eyes. His heart was pounding.

— What was that? — he whispered to himself.

He didn't know. But he knew it wasn't an ordinary premonition. It was _real_. More real than anything he had experienced in his life.

---
## II. THE DEVELOPMENT

A week later Marek sat at the conference "The Science of Consciousness" in Barcelona. The hall was filled with physicists, neuroscientists and philosophers who were trying to answer the question that had plagued humanity since the dawn of time: who are we?

He listened to successive papers about neural correlates, about integrated information theory, about the hard problem of consciousness. He knew all of it. He had written about it himself in his articles. But something inside him had broken.

_"This isn't the answer"_ – he thought. _"This is only describing the phenomenon. None of these explanations says what consciousness really is."_

During the break, an older man with warm eyes and a calm voice approached him.

— Professor Kowalski? — he asked. — My name is Federico. I've read your work. You're right about a lot of things, but you're thinking in the wrong direction.

— The wrong direction? — Marek raised an eyebrow. — And in which direction should I be thinking?

— Instead of looking for consciousness in the brain, you should start from consciousness as the foundation. What you study is only a shadow. The real reality is deeper.

Marek laughed.

— With all due respect, I've heard this many times. That's metaphysics, not science.

— And what if metaphysics is exactly science? — Federico smiled. — After all, quantum physics has shown that the world doesn't consist of separate parts. Everything is connected. Quantum entanglement, nonlocality... those aren't metaphors. They're facts. And you still think of consciousness as a product of the brain, while the brain is only a tool that consciousness uses to experience that reality.

Marek was silent. A memory from a week earlier appeared in his head – that strange experience of light and voice. _"You are part of the whole."_

— How do you know? — he asked.

— Because I've lived through it myself — answered Federico. — Once I was a materialist just like you. I thought everything could be explained by equations. Until one day I realised I was unhappy. And I asked myself: why? The answer came in the form of an experience that changed everything. I don't want to convert you. I only want to suggest that you try. Don't read about it in books. Live through it.

Marek wanted to answer, but Federico was already leaving.

— See you, professor — he said. — And remember: each of us must discover who he is by experiencing who he is. Not by reading other people's words.

---
## III. THE TURNING POINT

A month later Marek returned to Poland. The article was finished, but he felt it wasn't the end. Something inside him had broken. Something he couldn't fix.

He sat down in his study and opened a notebook. On the first page he wrote down the words Federico had said to him in Barcelona:

_"The one, the whole of everything that exists, has three properties: it is dynamic, it is holistic, and it wants to know itself."_

He read it three times. Then he closed the notebook and looked out the window. It was raining outside, but he didn't see the rain. He saw light. The same golden light he had seen in that vision.

He closed his eyes. And then, for the first time in years, he allowed himself to believe that there might be more to it than just science.

_"What if it's true?"_ – he thought. _"What if I'm not just a body? What if I'm part of something greater?"_

He got up and walked over to the mirror. He looked at his reflection for a long moment. In his eyes he saw something he hadn't seen in years: curiosity. Not that scientific, cool curiosity of a researcher. That real, childlike one, which asks: _"Who am I?"_

— I want to know — he said to his reflection. — I really want to know.

And then, as if in answer, he felt something he had never felt before. Love. Not for anyone in particular. Simply love. Flowing through him like a wave. And he understood that Federico had been right.

He wasn't a body. He was consciousness. A part of the whole. The oneness that was getting to know itself.

---
## IV. THE ENDING

A year later Marek no longer wrote articles about the neurobiology of consciousness. Instead he ran workshops for people who felt lost. He told them about oneness, about connection, about the fact that they were not alone.

— All my life I thought I was a body — he said to a hall full of people. — That all I had was a brain generating consciousness. And then I discovered that wasn't true. That I am part of something greater. And that each of you is too.

After the workshop, a woman with tear-filled eyes approached him.

— For thirty years I thought I was alone — she said. — That nobody understood me. And now I know... I know I am part of the whole.

Marek took her hand.

— You're returning to yourself — he said. — And that's the most important step.

He left the hall and looked at the sky. Somewhere in the distance the sun was breaking through the clouds. And for the first time in years Marek didn't feel that he was looking for answers.

He felt that he had finally found the question.

And that that was enough.

---
## V. EPILOGUE

A few months later Marek received a message from the woman from the workshop:

_"After that meeting I started meditating. For the first time in my life I felt that I wasn't alone. That I'm part of something greater. Thank you for showing me who I really am."_

Marek read the message three times. Then he put the phone in his pocket and went back to work.

There was another book before him, more workshops, more meetings with people who were looking for answers. But he knew there were no answers.

There is only the path.

And everyone who walks it returns to oneself.
