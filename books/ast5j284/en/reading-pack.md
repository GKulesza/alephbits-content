## Text

**RESPONSIBILITY**

## I. THE BEGINNING

Marek Kowalski stood in the middle of the open-plan office and looked at the clock. 11:47. In twenty minutes he was supposed to lay off the sixth person that week. Not the first time. Not the last.

His phone vibrated in his hand – his wife was writing about picking the kids up from kindergarten. He put it down. He didn't have time to answer.

— Mr. Marek? — he heard behind his back.

He turned around. A young woman stood before him, maybe in her mid-twenties, in an elegant jacket that tried to hide her age. She was holding a folder with documents.

— It's me, Kasia from HR. So... I'm supposed to hand you the documents.

Marek looked at the folder. He knew what was in it. It was the redundancy papers for Magda, his best employee.

— Thank you — he said, taking the folder. — I can do it myself.

Kasia nodded and left. Marek was left alone in the middle of the office that had been full of people a moment before.

---
## II. THE DEVELOPMENT

Marek opened the door to the conference room. Magda was sitting at the table, nervously fiddling with a pen. She was 27, had worked at the company for five years. She was his best analyst.

— Marek — she said when he came in. — Is this...?

— Sit down — he said, closing the door.

He sat down opposite her. For a moment he looked at his hands. Then at her.

— Magda, the company is going through a restructuring. You know that. We have to reduce the headcount by 15%.

— I know.

— Unfortunately, your position has been marked for elimination.

Magda looked at him for a moment. In her eyes he saw what he had seen before – pain that she was trying to hide.

— I understand — she said quietly. — But why me? Last month I got the best rating in the team.

Marek took a deep breath.

— It's not about your work. It's about the fact that your department is the least profitable. And your position is... repetitive.

— Repetitive? — Magda stood up. — Marek, I built this department from scratch! In five years I never took a single day of leave!

— I know. And I'm sorry.

— You're sorry? — her voice trembled. — You're telling me you're firing me because I'm "repetitive", and you're sorry?

Marek stayed silent. He knew that anything he said would only be an excuse.

— You have three months of severance — he said at last. — And a letter of recommendation.

— I don't need your letter — she answered, gathering her things. — I needed you to stand behind me. But you never stand behind anyone. You only ever carry out orders.

She left, leaving the door open.

Marek was left alone in the conference room. He looked at the empty chair where Magda had been sitting a moment before. And he understood that once again in his life he had chosen comfort over courage.

---
## III. THE TURNING POINT

A week later, Marek was sitting in his office. On the desk lay a stack of papers – more layoffs, more signatures, more conversations he didn't want to have.

The phone rang. His wife.

— I'm picking up the kids — she said. — But I'll be back later. I have something to take care of.

— What happened?

— Nothing. I just... have something to take care of.

She hung up.

Marek stood up and walked to the window. He looked at the city stretching out before him, and thought about Magda. About the fact that she had worked for him for five years. About the fact that he had failed her, because he was afraid to stand up to his superiors.

A memory surfaced in his mind, from a few years earlier, when his father had lost his job in similar circumstances. He remembered his face when he came home and said: "Son, you have no idea what it feels like when someone decides about your life and you have no say in it."

Marek closed his eyes.

— I'm sorry, Dad — he whispered.

---
## IV. THE ENDING

A month later, Marek returned to the same office, but this time he was no longer an employee. He had handed in his resignation a week earlier.

When his boss asked him why he was leaving, he answered:

— Because I don't want to be someone who carries out orders without thinking about the people those orders affect.

The boss looked at him with a mixture of surprise and respect.

— I understand — he said. — I respect your decision.

Marek left the building and looked at the sky. The sun was shining outside, and for the first time in years he felt that he had done something that mattered.

He got into the car and called his wife.

— I'm finishing up — he said. — I'm coming home.

— What happened?

— I resigned. I couldn't do it anymore.

— Really? — In her voice he heard something he hadn't heard in years – pride. — Are you sure?

— Yes. And you know what? It's the best decision I've ever made.

He started the engine and headed home. On the way he passed a café where Magda was sitting with a cup of coffee in her hand. He stopped and got out.

— Magda — he said, walking up to her. — I'm sorry. I know it doesn't change what happened. But I wanted you to know that...

— I know — she interrupted him. — I got a new job. Better paid. With better conditions.

Marek smiled.

— That's good. Really good.

— Thank you — she said. — For coming. And for apologizing.

— You're welcome.

He went back to the car and drove on. He knew this was not the end of his story. But for the first time in years he felt he was going in the right direction.

---
## V. THE EPILOGUE

A year later, Marek ran his own company – small, but honest. He employed ten people, including Magda, who had become his chief analyst. At the first meeting of the new team he said:

— I will demand a lot from you. But I will never do to you what was done to me. Not because I'm better. But because I know what it feels like when someone decides about your life and you have no say in it.

Magda smiled from the other end of the table.

— That's why I'm here — she said.

Marek nodded. He knew he had made the right decision. Not because it was easy. But because it was worth it.

---
**THE END**
