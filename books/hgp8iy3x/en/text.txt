Early in the morning I went out into the street and felt the city’s fresh air. The sun was only just rising over the rooftops of the tenement houses, and the Main Square was still quiet and empty.

I walked past the Cloth Hall and touched the cool stones of the wall. I thought of the hundreds of years of history that had seen the same cobbled streets and the same church bells.

In the Small Market Square I stopped by the well. The water in it was dark and calm like a mirror. Birds sang in the crowns of the trees, and I listened to their voices and rested for a moment.

Then I turned into a side street and saw a small café. The smell of fresh coffee mixed with the aroma of baked bread. I bought a roll and ate it slowly, watching the passers-by.

On the way to the Planty I heard street musicians. They were playing an old melody on violins that I knew from childhood. People stopped, smiled, and dropped small coins into an open suitcase.

Beyond the city walls I walked toward the Vistula. The river flowed calmly, carrying reflections of clouds and bridges. Boats were moored at the embankment, and the wind stirred the flags on the masts.

On the hill I saw Wawel. The white walls of the castle shone in the sun, and ravens circled above the towers. I felt proud that I could live so close to such an important place.

I went back down to the centre and entered a museum. In the rooms hung paintings of the city’s earlier life. Each painting told a different story about the people who had lived here.

In the evening I returned to the Main Square. Lanterns lit up one after another, and the tenement houses took on a golden glow. The city pulsed with life, and I felt part of that great story.

That day I learned that reading about a familiar place helps you remember new signs. Every word becomes a bridge between the language I know and the script I am learning.
