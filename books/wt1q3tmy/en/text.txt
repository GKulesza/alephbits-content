**PROLOGUE OF THE WIND**

When Yumiko first heard "Kaze no Prologue" on the radio, she was twenty-one years old and felt that she was standing on the threshold of something great. She did not yet know that this song – with its optimistic sound, with its lyrics about a wind that opens new chapters – would become one of her most important works. And that thirty years later, in 2026, people would still be watching it on YouTube, writing in the comments: "She used to be the most beautiful girl in Japan" and "Why did you recommend this to me only now?"

But that was still to come. For now she stood on the beach, the wind blowing through her hair, and the camera circled around her like a restless bird. The director was shouting something from a distance, but Yumiko could not hear him. She heard only the music playing in her earphones – and the wind, whispering to her ear about the future.

**The day before the shoot**

The evening before the music video shoot, Yumiko spent in her small apartment in Tokyo. On the table lay a note from her manager: "Tomorrow 5:00 AM, Chiba beach. Bring three changes of clothes – a white dress, a red shirt and something blue. The weather is supposed to be good."

Yumiko looked out of the window. The sky was clear, the stars shining brightly. The wind, which had raged over the capital for several days, had finally died down. She hoped tomorrow would be similar.

She was tired. The last weeks had been a whirlwind – interviews, photo sessions, studio recordings, meetings with fans. But it was this song, "Kaze no Prologue", that had something in it that gave her strength. The lyrics said that even in difficult moments you can always start anew. That the wind always blows, you only need to open the window.

She lay down on the bed and closed her eyes. The words were still ringing in her head:

_"Aoi takaku sora ga hirogaru no"_ – "The blue, high sky stretches out".

And she smiled.

**Chiba beach**

At dawn the beach was empty. Only the waves, the seagulls and the film crew, bustling around the equipment. Yumiko stood on the sand in a white dress that fluttered in the wind. The temperature was perfect – not too warm, not too cold. The wind, which had raged over Tokyo yesterday, was here gentle and friendly.

– Ready? – asked the director.

Yumiko nodded. She put in her earphones, heard the first bars of the song and began to walk towards the waves. She did not walk fast, did not walk slowly. She walked as if the wind itself were carrying her.

The cameras recorded her every move. A drone shot – although back then there were no drones, only a helicopter – showed the beach from above, with Yumiko as a small white speck against the blue sea. It was beautiful, but Yumiko was not thinking about how it looked. She was thinking about what she felt.

_"Kaze wo yobu toki, hajimaru no"_ – "When you call the wind, it begins".

And she felt that it was true. That every new challenge, every new song, every new day – is like calling the wind. It may not always answer, but there is always a chance that it will come.

**Afternoon in the studio**

After the beach session, Yumiko returned to the studio to record the vocals for the song. The song was almost ready, but the producer wanted her to add something of her own. She was not just to sing – she was to feel the words.

She stood behind the microphone, earphones on her ears, and watched through the glass the sound engineer giving her signals. She began to sing:

_"Dareka atte kowai no kana"_ – "Am I afraid that someone will hurt me?"

She closed her eyes. In her mind appeared an image of the beach, the wind, the waves. And suddenly she felt that this was not just a song. This was her life. All the doubts, all the fears, all the dreams – they were in those words.

The producer nodded. He was pleased.

– Once more – he said through the microphone. – But this time more... freely. As if you were talking to a friend.

Yumiko took a deep breath. And she sang again. But this time it was not singing. It was telling. Telling about the wind, about freedom, about the fact that even if you are afraid of the future, you can always start anew.

**The premiere**

When "Kaze no Prologue" was released in 1992, nobody expected it to become a hit. At that time, the Japanese music scene was dominated by big stars, and Yumiko – although talented – was not yet one of them. But the song had something in it that attracted people. Maybe it was the wind in the music video. Maybe Yumiko's smile. Maybe the lyrics that spoke of hope.

People began sending letters to radio stations. They asked where they could buy the record. They asked whether Yumiko would perform in their city. Suddenly she became more than just a singer. She became the voice of those who were looking for a new beginning.

In interviews she talked about what she had felt singing that song. About the wind that had always accompanied her. About the fact that every day is a new chance.

– I don't know what the future will bring – she once said on television. – But I know that if I listen to the wind, I will find the way.

**Thirty years later**

In 2026, when Japan and the whole world had changed beyond recognition, "Kaze no Prologue" was still present. Not on the radio, not on television – but in the hearts of those who remembered. Comments kept appearing on the Internet:

_"She used to be the most beautiful girl in Japan."_

_"Why did you recommend this to me only now?"_

_"This is a real idol, not like today's ones, made by AI."_

Yumiko, who was still alive, though she no longer sang as often, sometimes returned to those memories. She opened the wardrobe where the white dress hung – the same one she had worn on the beach in Chiba. She touched the fabric and felt the wind.

The wind, which was still blowing. And which was still calling her to new beginnings.

**Epilogue**

One evening, sitting in her house in Tokyo, Yumiko turned on YouTube. She found the music video for "Kaze no Prologue" – the same one she had filmed three decades earlier. She saw herself – young, smiling, with the wind in her hair. And she smiled.

She remembered what she had felt that day on the beach. That feeling that everything is possible. That every new day is a new chance. That the wind always blows, you only need to open the window.

She took a deep breath. And she felt that it was still true.

Even after all those years.

**THE END**
