**THE OBSERVER**

## I. THE BEGINNING

Michał Krawczyk was thirty-eight years old and had just lost everything.

Not literally, of course. His flat in Warsaw was still there, a few thousand zlotys remained in his account, and his mother called every week to ask whether he had found a "proper job" yet. But what he had lost was worse than money – he had lost certainty. He had lost the conviction that the world is predictable, that all you need to do is analyse the data well to know what is going to happen.

For the past five years he had traded on the cryptocurrency market. He started with small amounts, gradually built up his capital, developed a strategy that seemed to work. Charts, indicators, technical analysis – everything suggested he was on the right track. Until last month, when the market did something no model had predicted.

He was now sitting in his flat in Mokotów, looking at the computer screen on which the chart of his last trade was frozen. Loss: twenty-three thousand zlotys. Half a year of work.

– My analysis was wrong – he said to the empty room. – Simply... wrong.

Nobody answered him.

He got up and walked into the kitchen. On the counter lay a book he had bought a week ago, while passing an antiquarian bookshop. "The Golden Bough" by James George Frazer. He had picked it up for no reason, because the title sounded interesting, but he had not opened it until now. Now, with a void in his head, he reached for it.

He opened it at a random page and read:

_"In many ancient cultures there existed the motif of a god who dies and returns to life. It was a symbol of the cyclical nature of reality – the death of winter and the birth of spring, the end of the old order and the beginning of a new one."_

He rolled his eyes. More nonsense about superstitions. He put the book down, but he could not stop thinking about what he had read. _A god who dies and returns to life._ Wasn't that exactly what he felt? His old self had died – the self-confident trader who thought he controlled the market. Maybe now was the time for something new.

---
## II. THE DEVELOPMENT

The next day Michał got up at dawn. He had not slept well – that night he dreamed of charts dissolving into water, and of a figure in a white robe saying something about planets. It was such a strange dream that he decided to ignore it.

He went to the café on the corner, ordered a black coffee and sat by the window. He tried to read the news on his phone, but his gaze kept drifting to the street, where people walked to work, to schools, to their daily rituals.

– Excuse me, is this seat free?

He looked up. In front of him stood a woman in her forties – short red hair, glasses with thick frames, a smile that seemed both warm and attentive. In her hand she held a book on astrology.

– Yes, of course – he said, moving his bag aside.

The woman sat down, ordered tea and for a moment looked at him with curiosity.

– Michał, right? – she asked. – Marek told me about you. He said you have a problem with... the market.

– Marek? – he was surprised. – You know Marek?

– I know many people. – She smiled. – My name is Ewa. I'm an astrologer. And a psychologist.

Michał snorted with laughter. – Astrology? Really?

Ewa did not take offence. – I know what you're thinking. Newspaper horoscopes, mumbo jumbo, nonsense. I thought the same. For a long time.

– And what changed?

– What changed is that I began to treat astrology not as fortune-telling, but as a tool for understanding cycles. Rhythms. – She put her tea on the table. – Tell me, Michał, do you know why the market behaved the way it did?

– Because it's unpredictable – he answered gloomily. – Because it can't be controlled.

– And what if you didn't try to control it? What if, instead, you tried to understand its rhythm? – Ewa leaned forward. – You know, the ancient Mesopotamians believed that gods die and return to life. Dumuzi, Tammuz, Baal. That wasn't superstition. It was a metaphor for cyclicality. Summer and winter, growth and decline, life and death. Your loss is not a failure, Michał. It's winter. The dead season. But after winter, spring always comes.

Michał was silent. In his mind appeared the memory from last week, when he watched a film about the Strugatsky brothers. Someone said that in their novels the heroes often suffered defeats, but those defeats were the beginning of something new.

– What should I do? – he asked at last.

Ewa looked at him kindly.

– Stop trying to control the waves. Understand that there are cycles. And learn to surf them.

---
## III. THE TURNING POINT

Three weeks later Michał got up at five in the morning, as he had done every day for two weeks. He poured himself a coffee, opened his laptop and typed into the search engine: "planetary cycles and financial markets".

He found articles about Mercury retrograde, about the phases of the Moon, about how Jupiter and Saturn affect investors' decisions. He didn't believe in it. Not entirely. But there was something in it – something that resonated with what Ewa had said.

For three weeks he did not trade. He only observed. The market fell, then rose, then fell again – but he did not open positions. Instead he kept a journal in which he wrote down his emotions: fear, hope, uncertainty. What he had once considered weakness had now become data.

And then he remembered something he had read in a book on astrology: "The Saturn Return". A moment in life, usually around the age of thirty, when everything we have built falls apart into tiny pieces. Not a punishment, but a gift. A chance for a new beginning.

He was thirty-eight. Maybe he was going through his second Saturn return.

That morning, for the first time in a month, he opened a position. Not a big one – only symbolic. But he did it differently than before. Not with fever in his eyes, not with the conviction that he controlled the outcome. He did it with the acceptance that the outcome did not depend on him.

He sat in front of the screen and watched the price rise. Then fall. Then rise again. And he felt no fear.

– OK – he said to himself. – I don't know how this will end. But I know it's just a wave.

He closed the position after two hours with a profit of seven zlotys. It was a ridiculous amount. But he wasn't looking at the money. He was looking at the peace that had appeared in his head.

---
## IV. THE SECOND TURNING POINT

A few days later Michał received a message from Marek: _"I have something for you that will interest you. Let's meet on Saturday."_

They met in the same place – the café on the corner. Marek was an older man, a professor of history, who had been interested in the connections between mythology and psychology for years.

– You're right, Michał – said Marek, sipping his coffee. – Your loss is not a failure. It's part of a larger cycle. You know, in Mesopotamian mythology there was a god named Dumuzi. Every year he died and returned to life. But that was not just a myth – it was a way of describing reality. Death and rebirth, the end and a new beginning.

– And what does that have to do with my trading? – asked Michał, though deep down he felt the answer was somewhere close.

– Everything. – Marek leaned forward. – Your trading strategy, your analyses, your conviction that you can control the market – that was your old order. And it had to die so that something new could be born. But beware, Michał – this is not just a metaphor. In one of the books on astrology I read that in December 2020 a new cycle began. The great conjunction of Jupiter and Saturn in Aquarius. Two hundred years of the civilization of air. That means everything we knew ceases to apply. Our money, our values, our ways of thinking – everything is changing. And if you don't understand this cycle, you'll be left behind.

Michał was silent. In his mind appeared the memory of the past year – how everyone around him talked about artificial intelligence, about new technologies, about changes happening too fast to keep up.

– That means I have to change not only my strategy – he said slowly. – I have to change my way of thinking.

– Exactly – Marek confirmed. – And that is your Saturn return. Your chance for a new beginning.

---
## V. THE ENDING

Six months later Michał stood on the beach in Gdańsk, looking at the Baltic Sea. It was December, cold, but he did not feel the chill. Over the past six months he had been learning a new strategy – not based on technical analysis, but on understanding cycles. Planets, phases of the Moon, retrogrades – he did not treat them as an oracle, but as tools that helped him understand when to act and when to rest.

His account was slightly in the red – but not in the way that destroyed him. It was a minus he had learned to live with. He knew his limits, his rules, his cycles. He had stopped fighting the market. He had started observing it.

In his pocket he had a phone showing an astrological app – information that Jupiter had just entered the sign of Pisces, which was supposed to mark the beginning of a new emotional cycle.

He did not believe in it blindly. But he had learned to accept that there are things he does not understand. And that that is all right.

He remembered Ewa's words: _"Astrology steers nothing. It's just a clock. It shows you where you are on the timeline."_

And Marek's words: _"The greatest discoveries begin when someone notices something others don't want to see."_

He took a deep breath and looked at the sky. By day the stars were not visible, but he knew that somewhere out there, in space, the planets were circling in their rhythms. And that he, too, was part of that rhythm.

He smiled to himself.

_"Good"_ – he thought. _"This is exactly how it should be."_

He walked along the shore, leaving behind a past that had to die so that something new could be born. Because he knew that what really mattered was only just beginning.

---
## VI. THE EPILOGUE

At night Michał woke up with a strange feeling. He dreamed he was standing in an ancient temple, and before him stood a priest speaking of Tammuz. Of the god who dies and returns to life. But in that dream it was not a myth – it was reality.

He sat up in bed and reached for his phone. He opened the astrological app and checked the position of the planets. Venus was just entering a trine with Jupiter – an arrangement which, as he had been told, favoured new beginnings.

He got up, poured himself some tea and sat down in front of the computer. He opened his trading journal and typed:

_"Today I begin a new cycle. I don't know what it will bring. But I know I am ready."_

He closed the computer and looked out the window. Outside, snow was beginning to fall – the first snow of this winter. White flakes fell slowly, covering the city with a layer of silence.

He remembered the words he had read in Frazer's book: _"The god dies so that he may be born anew."_

And he understood that it had not been only a metaphor.

It had been a plan.
