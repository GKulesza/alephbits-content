## Text

**CAMERA ON THE STREET**


## I. January 1st, 4:55 in the morning

When Tom came to in the middle of the night, he did not at once know where he was. He was still wearing his New Year's Eve clothes, there was a taste of champagne in his mouth and a buzzing in his head that would not go away. Beside him lay an empty space. Ana had not come back to bed. She usually slept on the left side, her head turned toward him, even when they quarrelled. Now there was nobody there.

He sat up and for a moment listened to the silence. The house was quiet, too quiet. The children were sleeping in their rooms, and he was alone with the noise building up in his head.

He got up and went to the kitchen. Ana was lying on the floor.

She was not moving. Her eyes were open, but they did not see. Tom looked at her, and then he sat down on a chair and began to think. It was a strange moment, in which something inside him stopped. As if his heart had stopped beating, and then began to beat the other way.

He stood up, went to the wardrobe and took a phone out of it. He did not call for help. Instead he opened the browser and began to type.

_"After what time does a body begin to emit a smell?"_

A moment later:

_"How to stop the decomposition process?"_

And then:

_"How to embalm a body?"_

His fingers moved on their own, as if they belonged to someone else. As if something in him had taken control. And he did not know whether it was fear, or something worse.

---

## II. New Year's Eve

The guest left at 1:30. Tom closed the door behind him and returned to the living room, where Ana was standing by the window.

— It was nice — she said, without turning around.

— It was — he answered.

For a moment they were silent. Outside the window fireworks could be seen over the bay. The new year had begun with a bang and colours that lit up the night sky.

— We need to talk — Ana said at last.

— About what?

— About William.

Tom felt something gripping his throat.

— William? That broker?

— Yes.

She turned and looked at him. In her eyes there was something Tom had not seen for years — determination. And fear.

— I know that you know — she said. — I saw you looking at my phone. I know you saw the messages.

— Ana...

— Don't interrupt me. I wanted to tell you myself. I've been preparing for this for weeks.

Tom walked over to her and sat down on the sofa. He felt the floor giving way under his feet, as if the whole life they had built were only a house of cards that the wind was about to blow away.

— I told you that if something was wrong, we would deal with it together — he said quietly.

— I know. And I wanted to. But I was afraid of how you'd react.

— And how was I supposed to react?

Ana sat down next to him and took his hand.

— I don't know. I thought you'd be angry. That you'd shout. That you'd tell me I was destroying our family.

— And you? What do you think?

— I think I fell into something I don't understand. That William is only a symptom of something that has been in our marriage for a long time.

— Which means what? My fault?

— Not yours. Ours. Both of ours. We've talked about it so many times, but we never changed anything. We're like two ships sailing alongside each other, but never getting close enough to dock.

Tom was silent for a long moment. Then he stood up and walked to the window.

— Does he know that you know? — he asked.

— No. And I don't want him to. This has to stay between us. This is our matter, not his.

Tom turned and looked at her. She was beautiful, even now, when her face was tense with emotion. She had always been beautiful. And she had always been beyond his reach, even when she stood next to him.

— Fine — he said. — Our matter. We'll deal with it. But tomorrow. It's too late now.

Ana nodded and stood up.

— I'm going to bed — she said. — Good night.

— Good night.

She went to the bedroom. Tom stayed in the living room, watching the fireworks slowly dying out over the bay. He felt that something was ending. Not just the year. Something bigger. And he did not know if he had the strength to stop it.

---

## III. January 1st, 6:25

Tom returned to the kitchen. Ana was still lying on the floor.

He picked her up in his arms and carried her down to the basement. The body was heavier than he thought. Heavier than when he held her. Heavier than the life they had carried together.

In the basement he laid her on the concrete floor and covered her with a tarpaulin he found in the toolbox. Then he sat down on the stairs and began to think.

He knew he had to act quickly. But something in him said to stop. To think. To plan every move.

He took out the iPad that was lying on the living room table — it belonged to one of the children. He opened the browser.

_"What is formaldehyde and how does it work?"_

_"How long does DNA last?"_

_"Is it possible to identify a person on the basis of incomplete remains?"_

He felt that all of this was like a dream. As if he were watching a film about someone else, not about himself. And in that dream he was calm. Too calm. As if his mind had separated itself from what he was doing.

The next day he drove to a shop.

A mop, brushes, tape, a tarpaulin, a protective suit, buckets, goggles, baking soda. And a small axe.

He bought all of it for cash. He put on a mask and gloves. And he did not stop once.

---

## IV. The investigation

Detective Mark Riley sat in his office and looked at the surveillance pictures. In one of them a masked man could be seen throwing bags into a dumpster. In another — the same man, this time without a mask, in a shop.

— That's him — he said to his partner. — Brian Walsh.

— Are you sure?

— As sure as I am that I'm here. Look at those purchases. Check the search history on the tablet. This is not random.

The partner looked at the printouts.

— This is fucking cold — he said. — Someone who plans this isn't acting in panic.

— Exactly. He isn't acting in panic. He's acting step by step. He knew what he was doing.

Riley stood up and walked to the window. Behind him stretched the city he was supposed to protect. And in it, somewhere out there, was a man who had murdered his wife, and then dismembered her and scattered her remains in several rubbish bins.

— I need more — he said. — I need to know where the body is.

— We won't find it. He was too careful.

— Don't be so sure. People always make mistakes. Even those who think they're perfect.

And indeed, Brian made one mistake. He forgot that in his house, in the basement, there were tools. And that on one of them — on the axe — traces of blood would be found.

Not her blood. His own. But that was enough to connect him to the scene of the crime. And to prove that Ana had not died in her sleep.

She had been killed.

And he, Brian, had been there.

---

## V. The trial

The courtroom was full. Brian sat on the defendant's chair, dressed in a grey long-sleeved shirt, calm, composed. Beside him stood his lawyer, who from the start repeated one thing: Ana died suddenly in her sleep, and Brian panicked.

— This was not murder — said the lawyer. — It was an accident. And what came after was desperation.

The prosecutor was not convinced.

— If it was an accident, why didn't Brian call an ambulance? Why instead did he search the internet for how to dismember a body? Why did he buy tools and cleaning products? Why did he lie to the police?

The courtroom fell silent. The prosecutor walked over to the jury box.

— Ana was not a stranger to the defendant. She was his wife. The mother of his three children. And he murdered her.

The judge looked at Brian for the whole duration of the hearing. There was no pity in her eyes.

— The way you treated your wife's body shows a complete lack of respect. Not only toward her, but also toward your children.

Brian did not answer. He had nothing to say.

---

## VI. The verdict

When he heard the words "life imprisonment", Brian did not react. He did not flinch, did not blink. He sat like a statue, with a face that betrayed no emotion.

On the other side of the room, in the last row, sat Milanka, Ana's mother. She was not crying. She had an expression that said everything — that her daughter was gone, that no one would bring her back, that justice is only a word.

Ana's sister stood up and said one sentence:

— Justice has been served.

Then she left the room, without looking back.

---

## VII. A few weeks later

Tom, really Brian, sat in his cell and looked at the wall. Light came in through the window, but he did not see it. He saw only his hands. The hands that had killed. The hands that were supposed to touch the children. The hands that would never touch anyone again.

He did not know whether he regretted it. Maybe inside, somewhere deep, somewhere where his heart had been, he felt something like regret. But he could not name it. He felt indifferent.

Everything was indifferent to him.

Somewhere out there, in Massachusetts, his children were growing up without parents. Somewhere out there, in Serbia, Ana's mother was still waiting for a phone call that would never come.

And he, Brian, was here. And he had a life sentence.

And he felt nothing.

---


**THE END**
