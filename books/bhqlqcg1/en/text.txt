**INTELLECTUAL FROTTEURISM**

## I. THE BEGINNING

Ewa sat on the sofa, staring at the television screen, on which a programme was playing that she wasn't watching. In her hand she held a phone, and on the screen was an app she had opened automatically, without thinking about why. Her husband, Marek, sat beside her and read a book. For forty minutes neither of them had said a word.

It was not that good silence, shared by two people who don't need to say anything to feel close. It was a silence that grew like a wall between them.

Ewa put down the phone and looked at Marek. He was 52, grey-haired, wore reading glasses and had wrinkles around his eyes that had appeared over the last ten years. She had known him for twenty-five. She thought she knew everything about him. And now, looking at him, she realised that she no longer knew who he was.

— Marek — she said.

He looked up from his book.

— Yes?

— Do you ever feel that we no longer know each other?

Marek put the book down. For a moment he looked at her in silence.

— I do — he answered. — And I wonder whether that's normal. Whether every relationship looks like this after so many years. Whether it's just... routine.

— Or maybe it's not routine — said Ewa. — Maybe it's something else. Maybe we've stopped listening to each other.

— What do you mean?

— When you say something you've already said, I think: "Here we go again". And instead of listening to what you're really saying, I listen to my own thought, which says: "Here we go again". And then I don't hear you. I hear myself.

Marek smiled sadly.

— I do the same. When you say you want something, I think: "She wants this because she wants to control me". And instead of listening to what you're really saying, I listen to my fear of ceasing to be myself.

Ewa took his hand.

— What do we do?

— I don't know. — Marek looked at her. — But maybe we should start by stopping guessing what the other thinks. And start asking.

---
## II. THE DEVELOPMENT

The next day Ewa woke up at 5:47. She got up, drank her coffee and sat down at the computer. In the browser she opened a tab with a podcast recommended to her by a colleague. The topic: "How to achieve mastery in romantic relationships".

The guest was a man named Miłosz, who spoke quickly, with humour, and in a way that made her feel as if he were talking to her, not to a million listeners.

_"A human being has a built-in script"_ – she heard. _"When someone deceives you, your first thought is not 'deceiver', but 'God has finally found me'."_

Ewa froze. That was exactly what she had felt when she met Marek. She thought she had finally found someone who would understand her. And for twenty-five years she had believed it was true.

And now?

_"The longer we know someone, the more we begin to dig into what he means by that proposal"_ – Miłosz continued. _"Instead of wondering whether the proposal is good, we think: what are his intentions?"_

Ewa closed the laptop. A memory from a week earlier appeared in her head — Marek had proposed a trip to the mountains, and she had thought: "He wants to take me away from here, because he doesn't want to talk to me". And he had only wanted to spend time with her.

— I built this wall — she whispered to herself. — Not him. Me.

---
## III. THE TURNING POINT

Ewa came home late in the evening. Marek was waiting for her in the kitchen with dinner – her favourite dish, which he cooked only when he wanted to apologise. And suddenly she understood that for years she had been misreading his gestures.

When he cooked her favourite dish, she thought: "He's done something wrong again". And he simply wanted to give her pleasure.

— Marek — she said, sitting down at the table. — I'd like to ask you something.

— Yes?

— Last time you cooked this dish, were you apologising to me for something?

Marek looked at her in surprise.

— No. I cooked it because I knew you liked it. And I wanted you to have a good evening.

Ewa felt like crying.

— And all along I thought it was a sign that you'd done something wrong.

Marek took her hand.

— It's us, Ewa. It's our relationship. Over so many years we've learned to read each other, but we read wrongly. We read what we're afraid of, not what's really there.

— How do we change that?

— Maybe we start by accepting that we don't know. And that what we think may be wrong. And ask.

— Ask?

— Yes. Instead of thinking: "He did it because he wants to hurt me", ask: "Why did you do it?"

Ewa smiled.

— That sounds simple.

— It's not simple — he answered. — But maybe it's worth trying.

---
## IV. THE ENDING

A year passed. Ewa and Marek still quarrelled. They still had days when they couldn't stand the sight of each other. But they had learned one thing: they had stopped guessing.

When Ewa said: "You forgot the rubbish again", and Marek answered: "I didn't forget, I just didn't get to it", she no longer thought: "He's lying again". Instead she said: "Why didn't you get to it?"

And she listened.

And when Marek said: "I'd like to go to the mountains", Ewa no longer thought: "He wants to run away from me". Instead she asked: "Why do you want to go?"

And she listened.

They discovered that over twenty-five years each of them had become someone different. And that what had united them at the beginning didn't have to be what would unite them to the end.

And what united them was not knowledge about each other. It was the willingness to keep discovering.

One evening, as they sat on the sofa, Marek said:

— You know, I've been thinking about what you said back then, when it all began. That we'd stopped listening to each other. And I think you were right. But now I think this is not the end. This is the beginning.

Ewa looked at him.

— Of what?

— Of something new. Something we don't yet know.

She smiled.

— That sounds like a plan.

---
## V. THE EPILOGUE

A few years later Ewa wrote a letter to her younger sister, who had just got married:

_"The biggest mistake I made in my marriage was thinking that I knew my husband inside out. That I knew what he would say before he even opened his mouth. Only when I stopped guessing and started asking did I see that all along he was someone I could still discover. And that was the most beautiful discovery of my life. Don't be afraid of not knowing. Be afraid of thinking you know."_

The sister called her a week later.

— I read your letter. And you know what? I thought I knew my husband. But after what you wrote, I understood that I don't know him at all. I only know what I think about him.

— And what are you going to do about it?

— Ask him who he really is. And listen.

Ewa smiled at the phone.

— That's a good beginning.
