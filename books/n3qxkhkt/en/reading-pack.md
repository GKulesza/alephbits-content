## Text

**THE CABBAGE SECRET**

Kacper was eleven years old and knew one thing: his grandma was the most boring person in the world. And she smelled of cabbage. Not the ordinary kind, boiled for dinner. It was a specific smell — sour, musty, as if someone had left a pot of cabbage soup for a week in a closed cupboard. Every Friday, when his parents went to their "dance evenings", Kacper ended up in the same place: in a tiny house on a pensioners' estate, where time flowed to the rhythm of an old clock's ticking and the crackling of grandma's hearing aid.

– Your soup has gone cold – said grandma, putting a bowl in front of him. – But that's fine, it's still warm.

Kacper looked at the pale green sludge with pieces of cabbage floating in it. He knew he had to eat. He also knew that in a moment grandma would offer him seconds. And then another helping.

– Thank you, grandma – he said, picking up his spoon. – It's delicious.

He lied. But he had learned that sometimes a lie is easier than the truth.

Grandma smiled through her glasses and sat down opposite him. She put her portion of soup into her mouth, and Kacper watched her chew for a good two minutes before she swallowed. In the meantime, a sound resembling a siren wailing came from her hearing aid.

– How's school? – she asked.

– Boring – he muttered.

Grandma nodded, as if that were the best answer in the world. Then she pulled an old, worn handkerchief from the cuff of her woollen cardigan, dipped it in a glass of water and, before Kacper could move away, wiped his face.

– Your face is dirty – she said. – Like a little child's.

Kacper groaned inwardly. He was eleven years old. He wasn't a little child. But grandma had always spoken to him like that. As long as he could remember.

After the soup came the cake. Cabbage cake. Of course.

– Your favourite – said grandma, placing a slice on his plate.

Kacper looked at it. It resembled a green brick. Inside, between the layers of cake, there was a sour filling that looked like something that should have gone into the compost bin.

He ate it. Swallowed. Smiled.

– Yum – he said. – Delicious.

– Good – replied grandma. – There's still half a tray left.

Kacper felt his stomach turn.

---
After dinner, when grandma went to the kitchen to do the dishes, Kacper slipped into the living room. His goal was the book corner. Grandma had loads of books – crime novels about gangsters, robbers, the mafia. They lay scattered on shelves, windowsills, under chairs. Kacper loved looking at the covers. On each one there was someone with a gun, or a fast car, or a beautiful woman in a red dress.

He wondered where such a boring, cabbage-smelling grandma got a taste for such dangerous stories.

– Like it? – he suddenly heard a voice right by his ear.

Kacper jumped. Grandma was standing behind him, wiping her hands on her apron. In her hand she held an old, yellowed book.

– This is my favourite – she said. – "The Great Treasure". About a boy who found old maps in the cellar…

– Grandma, do you read this? – asked Kacper, unable to hide his surprise.

Grandma looked at him the way one looks at someone who has just revealed they don't believe in Santa Claus.

– I read, dear. And you?

Kacper looked down. He didn't like reading. He couldn't. He had to repeat a year at school because he couldn't keep up. The teachers said he was "lazy" and "didn't apply himself". But Kacper knew that wasn't true. He simply couldn't. The letters arranged themselves into words that didn't want to arrange themselves into sentences. For others it was easy. For him it was like climbing the highest mountain.

– A little – he lied.

Grandma hugged him tightly. She smelled of cabbage, soap and old age.

– We can read together – she suggested. – I'll read aloud, and you listen.

Kacper wanted to refuse. But in her voice he heard something he had never heard before – a note of sadness. Loneliness. Suddenly he understood that grandma had no one but him. And those books. And that soup.

– All right – he said. – But later.

---
Later came sooner than Kacper expected. Grandma took an old leather map out of the wardrobe and spread it on the table. Kacper stared at it in disbelief.

– Is that from a book? – he asked.

– No, dear – replied grandma. – This is something real. From my days.

And then Kacper saw that the map was covered with notes. Not printed – handwritten. Marks. Arrows. Names of places that appeared on no modern map.

– Grandma, who are you? – he whispered.

The old lady smiled mysteriously. In her eyes appeared a gleam that Kacper had never seen before.

– I was someone else once, dear – she said. – But that's a long story. And it's better if no one ever finds out about it.

Kacper didn't know what to think. But he knew one thing: his boring, cabbage-smelling grandma was hiding a secret. And that secret smelled of something far more exciting than soup.

– I'm staying here for the night – he said. – And that's final. Mum and Dad can get offended, but I'm not leaving until you tell me everything.

Grandma looked at him for a long moment. Then she took an old, crumpled photograph out of her pocket. In the picture was a young woman in a leather jacket, standing next to a big black motorcycle. Beside her stood a tall man in a hat, with a smile that looked like it came straight out of a gangster film.

– Is that you, grandma? – asked Kacper, not believing his own eyes.

– Yes, dear. That's me. And that was the beginning of my greatest adventure.

---
Grandma told him about the old days, about treasures, about escapes, about night-time chases. About how she met grandpa – the one in the hat, who was the most famous robber in the whole country. About how they planned the greatest heist in history together, and then... decided to settle down.

– Why did you stop? – asked Kacper.

Grandma looked at him tenderly.

– For you – she said. – For the family. For your safety. We couldn't risk someone hurting you.

Kacper felt something tighten in his throat. All his life he had thought grandma was boring. And it turned out she was a heroine. That for years she had given up everything for him and his parents.

– Grandma – he said. – You're amazing.

The old lady smiled and hugged him tightly. She smelled of cabbage. But this time Kacper didn't mind at all.

– And now, dear – she said. – I'll show you something no one else has ever seen.

And she took out a small silver key from behind the picture on the wall. A key that — as it turned out — opened the door to the greatest secret he had ever known.

---
That night Kacper didn't go home. He stayed at grandma's. And even though it still smelled of cabbage inside, and the clock ticked as loudly as ever, everything was different. His grandma wasn't boring. She was the coolest person in the world. And he hoped that one day, when he grew up, he would be like her.

Brave. Mysterious. And ready for any adventure.

Even if he smelled of cabbage.

**THE END**
