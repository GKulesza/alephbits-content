## Text

**The Dog Pankracy and Important Matters**

Franek was seven years old and had one worry that grew along with him – he was the weakest in his class. Not in maths, not in reading, but when it came to what happened in the yard. When the older boys played football, they always picked him last. When they raced, he always stayed behind. And when they once tried to take away his mid-morning snack, he couldn't defend himself. He just stood there and watched the cheese sandwich travel into somebody else's backpack.

He didn't have a big brother. Well, he did, but only the kind that lived far away – in another city, with his dad, because his parents had separated. His brother came every few months, brought presents, talked about high school, and then disappeared again. That wasn't the same as having someone around every day. Someone who waits outside school and says: "Leave him alone."

That's why Franek had come to love Pankracy so much.

Pankracy wasn't his dog. He belonged to the neighbour on the ground floor, Mr Zygmunt – an elderly gentleman who moved with a cane and called everyone "my dear". But ever since Franek could remember, Pankracy spent more time with him than with his owner. He waited by the door when Franek came home from school. He laid his head on Franek's knees while he did his homework. And always, but always, he stood between Franek and those older boys whenever they got too pushy.

Pankracy was shaggy, grey-brown, and had a patch over his right eye, which made him look a bit like a pirate. His tail was like a separate creature – always in motion, always wagging, as if it wanted to say: "Come on, look, the world is interesting." And Franek went. Across the meadow, through the meadow, along the narrow forest paths – wherever the dog's paws led.

One Saturday morning, while everyone was still asleep, Franek slipped out of the flat and knocked on Mr Zygmunt's door.

– My dear! – the old man called out as he opened the door. – And where are you off to so early?

– Can Pankracy come for a walk with me? – asked Franek.

Mr Zygmunt looked at him thoughtfully.

– Pankracy, you say... And do you know why he's called that?

Franek shook his head.

– Then come in, and I'll tell you.

They sat down in the armchairs, and Pankracy settled between them on the carpet, as if he knew they were going to talk about him. Mr Zygmunt took an old cassette tape out of the drawer – the kind Franek had never seen before.

– This is a recording from the programme "Piątek z Pankracym". A long time ago, when I was still young and your dad was your age, we used to listen to it together. And you know what? I remember every song.

He turned the tape to the right side and put it into the old cassette player. After a moment, music came from the speaker – a little crackly, a little distant, but cheerful.

"In just a little while, in just a moment, Piątek z Pankracym will start to spin..."

Franek listened with his mouth open. The song was about a dog. The same dog that was now lying on the carpet! Well, maybe not the same one – that Pankracy was a cartoon, a bit different – but still, the same. Someone, long ago, had come up with a dog that liked children, chased pigeons, chewed slippers and sometimes had fleas. A dog that was a friend.

And then came the song about a big brother.

"When a person is still quite young, it's very hard to walk against the wind... So every little one knows from the cradle how much a big brother is needed."

Franek listened and felt something tighten in his throat. He looked at Pankracy. The dog lifted his head, as if he understood.

– You know, my dear – said Mr Zygmunt, seeing his face – a big brother isn't always someone who has the same parents. Sometimes it's someone who is just there. And looks after you. Like Pankracy.

Franek thought about how Pankracy stood between him and the boys in the yard. About how once, when he tripped and scraped his knee, Pankracy licked the wound and didn't leave his side until he got home.

– So Pankracy is my big brother? – he asked.

Mr Zygmunt laughed.

– Well, he's a bit shaggy for a brother. And on four paws. But yes, my dear. Pankracy loves you. And that's what counts.

That winter something strange happened. A heavy snow fell – so heavy it covered the whole yard, and someone had made a snowman on the lawn in front of the block of flats. Franek, walking home from school with Pankracy at his side, saw it first.

The snowman was almost as tall as him. He had coal eyes, a carrot nose and an old pot on his head instead of a hat. He looked almost alive.

Pankracy walked up to him, sniffed him, and then barked – once, twice, three times. But the snowman stood still.

– Why isn't he moving? – asked Franek.

Pankracy tilted his head. He touched the snowman with his paw, but pulled it back right away – the snow was cold. He looked at Franek, as if saying: "And now what?"

– Maybe he's hibernating? – the boy wondered.

Pankracy sat down and started to think. Franek sat down too. And so the two of them sat – the boy and the dog – looking at the motionless snowman, until a cawing sounded from the tree above them.

It was a crow. Old, grey on the wings, the kind that had seen everything and was afraid of nothing.

– What are you thinking about, Pankracy? – she cawed.

The dog barked.

– He's thinking about why the snowman doesn't move – Franek answered for him.

The crow tilted her head.

– And why should he move? It's only snow, after all.

– But he looks alive! – Franek protested.

– Looking and being are two different things – replied the crow, and flew away.

Franek and Pankracy looked at each other. Then at the snowman. Then at each other again.

– I guess not everything that looks alive is really alive – said the boy.

Pankracy wagged his tail, as if he agreed.

And then Mr Zygmunt arrived, looking for Pankracy to take him home. He saw the two of them sitting in front of the snowman and just shook his head with a smile.

– Philosophers, my dear. In a moment you'll start reading Plato.

Franek didn't know who Plato was. But he already knew something important: that there are things in the world that look frightening – like the older boys in the yard – but that really can't do anything to you if you have someone brave by your side. And there are things that look alive – like the snowman – but really aren't. And there are friends who will never leave you, even if they have four paws and sometimes chew your slippers.

In the evening, before bed, Franek asked his mum:

– Can Pankracy be my big brother?

Mum looked at him, surprised.

– I mean... you do have a big brother, sweetheart. He lives with dad, but...

– I know – Franek interrupted. – But he's not here. And Pankracy is.

Mum sat down on the edge of the bed and stroked his head.

– You know what? I think Pankracy would be honoured.

The next day, when Franek went out into the yard, the same older boys were there again. The one who had taken his sandwich started walking towards him again. But this time Pankracy stood at Franek's side. He didn't growl. He didn't bark. He just stood – shaggy, grey-brown, with the patch over his eye – and watched.

The boy stopped.

– We're going – he said to Franek, as if the dog weren't there at all.

And then Pankracy took a step forward. Just one. But it was enough. The boy stepped back and muttered something under his breath.

Franek couldn't believe it. All that time he had thought he needed a big brother who would defend him with his fists. And all it took was one step. One gesture. One look.

– Thank you – he whispered to the dog.

Pankracy licked his hand and wagged his tail.

And then Franek heard in his head the words of the song that Mr Zygmunt had played on the old cassette player. About how good it is to walk through the world when you have a big brother beside you. That then the wind stops blowing, and you can even laugh in the face of a lion.

Pankracy wasn't human. He couldn't talk, he couldn't solve maths problems, he couldn't play football – at least not without tearing the ball to shreds. But he was there. He was simply there. And that was the most important thing.

Because sometimes a big brother isn't the one who can do everything. Only the one who is close.

**THE END**
