**A KINDERGARTEN WITHOUT WALLS**

Marta had been a kindergarten teacher for fifteen years. She loved children, but she felt that something in her job had broken. When she started, she had fire in her, ideas, joy in every day. Now she only felt tired. Following the core curriculum, filling in monthly plans, making sure the children sat at their desks and coloured inside the lines – it all drained her energy.

She sat in the classroom, looking at a group of four-year-olds. The children sat in a little circle, and she showed them a colouring sheet with an apple.

– Colour the apple red – she said. – And don't go outside the line.

Little Franek raised his hand.

– Miss Marta, why does the apple have to be red? Can mine be purple?

Marta looked at him. In his eyes shone the curiosity that was extinguished every day in this system, in which every answer was predetermined.

– Franek, apples are red – she answered. – That's how it is in the books.

Franek lowered his eyes and started colouring the apple red, but he did it without enthusiasm. Marta felt that she had just killed something important in him.

---
That evening Marta couldn't fall asleep. She tossed and turned in bed, thinking about work, about the children, about herself. She turned on her laptop and started looking for inspiration. She came across a conference on alternative methods of working with children in kindergarten. She registered without hesitation.

A few days later she sat in a lecture hall, listening to two women – Grażyna and Agata. They talked about their kindergarten, about how they work with children outside, how they follow their ideas, how they develop key competences in a natural way.

– We don't hold classes in a closed room – said Grażyna. – The children spend most of their time outdoors, whatever the weather. They learn through experience, through play, through contact with nature.

– And how do you implement the core curriculum? – someone from the audience asked.

Agata smiled.

– We implement it along the way. When the children are interested in dinosaurs, they organise a conference about dinosaurs. When they want to know where rain comes from, we do experiments. Maths? We count leaves, sticks, pebbles. Language? The children listen, talk, tell stories. Everything happens naturally.

Marta felt something break inside her. It was exactly what she had been looking for.

---
After the conference Marta went up to Grażyna and Agata.

– I've been a teacher for fifteen years – she said. – And I feel stuck. I'd like to work the way you do, but I don't know how to start.

Agata looked at her warmly.

– Start with small steps – she said. – Go outside with the children. See what interests them. Follow them. You don't have to change everything at once.

Grażyna added:

– And remember: children are experts on themselves. They know what they want to learn. Your job is to accompany them in that, not to impose ready-made solutions.

Marta went home with her head full of ideas.

---
The next day Marta entered the classroom with a smile.

– Today we're going outside – she said. – The whole day. We'll play, learn and discover the world.

The children looked at her in disbelief. Then they started to cheer.

Outside, Marta let the children do what they wanted. She didn't impose topics, she didn't tell them what to do. She observed.

– Miss Marta, why is the sky blue? – asked Zosia.

Marta sat down next to her.

– I don't know – she answered. – But we can try to discover it together.

They organised a little experiment. They took a glass of water, a mirror and a torch. The children watched how the light split into colours. Zosia was delighted.

– And why does it rain? – asked Franek.

– I don't know – answered Marta. – But we can find out.

The children started collecting leaves, sticks, pebbles. They made a rain gauge out of them. They watched how the water gathered in the container.

Marta saw in their eyes the same light she had seen long ago, when she started working. Curiosity. Wonder. Joy.

---
Weeks passed. Marta changed the way she worked. She no longer held traditional lessons. Instead she organised projects that grew out of the children's interests.

When Franek brought a toy dinosaur to kindergarten, Marta asked:

– Do you want to learn more about dinosaurs?

The children shouted: "Yes!"

And so the "Dinosaurs" project began. The children gathered information, made drawings, organised a conference to which they invited their parents. Each child was an expert on a different dinosaur. Marta only helped them.

– It's amazing – said one of the parents after the conference. – My child has never been so engaged in learning.

Marta smiled.

– Because it's not learning – she answered. – It's play. And when a child plays, they learn best.

---
One day Marta invited Franek's dad to the kindergarten. He was a veterinarian. He told the children about animals and showed them the tools he uses at work. The children asked lots of questions.

– How do you treat a lion? – someone asked.

Franek's dad smiled.

– Fortunately, I don't treat lions – he answered. – But I can tell you about dogs and cats.

The children listened with bated breath. Then they wanted to be veterinarians themselves. Marta organised a veterinary corner where the children "treated" stuffed animals. Along the way they learned responsibility, empathy, cooperation.

The parents were delighted.

– Our child talks about kindergarten every day – said Zosia's mum. – She doesn't want to come home. She finally likes learning.

Marta knew that this wasn't her achievement alone. It was the result of work based on openness, on following the child, on natural learning through experience.

---
At the end of the school year Marta organised a meeting for parents. She told them about the methods she had used during the year. About how important working outside is, how key competences develop, how children become independent and responsible.

The parents listened attentively. Then Franek's mum stood up and said:

– Miss Marta, I wanted to thank you. My son used to not want to go to kindergarten. Now he doesn't want to leave. You changed his attitude to learning. And our attitude to parenting.

Marta felt tears welling up in her eyes.

– It's you who changed your attitude – she answered. – I only showed you that it can be done differently.

---
Today Marta is a different teacher. She still works in the kindergarten, but she no longer feels tired. Every day is an adventure for her. She isn't afraid of challenges, she isn't afraid of mistakes. She knows that the most important thing is that children feel safe, loved and interested in the world.

One day, as she was walking home, she heard a voice behind her:

– Miss Marta! Miss Marta!

She turned around. It was Franek, now a first-grader. He ran up to her and hugged her.

– Miss Marta, do you remember how we learned about dinosaurs?

– I remember, Franek.

– That was my favourite day in kindergarten. And now at school I like learning too. Because I know I can ask questions. And that every answer is interesting.

Marta hugged him tighter.

– That's the most beautiful thing you could have told me – she whispered.

And then she understood that what she does makes sense. That every child she helped discover the joy of learning is her success. That education is not filling in worksheets. It's opening minds.

And that that's what it's all about.

**THE END**
