**THE THICK JOURNAL**

When Patryk opened his eyes, the first thing he saw was the ceiling. White, smooth, perfectly empty. Like his life, he thought. Then he heard the alarm on his phone. He was supposed to get up, go for a run, make breakfast, drive to work. Instead, he turned on his side and stared at the screen.

In the first five minutes of his consciousness he managed to see: three reels about how he should be starting his day, two stories from friends on holiday in Dubai, a post about a diet that changed someone's life, and a quote from David Goggins about how "most people die at the age of twenty-five and are buried at seventy-five".

He closed his eyes.

– I'm thirty-four – he said to himself. – And I feel like a hundred.

---

Patryk's job was to design systems meant to make other people more efficient. The irony that he himself couldn't even organise his own wardrobe didn't escape him. In the office he was that guy who always smiled, always knew what to say in a meeting. Outside the office he was the guy who sat in his car in front of the building for twenty minutes before going inside.

– This isn't normal – his sister Magda once told him. – It's not normal for a man in his thirties to be afraid to enter his own apartment.

– I'm not afraid – he answered. – I just... don't feel like it.

– Feeling like living?

– Like anything.

Magda gave him a strange look then. After a moment she said:

– You know that guy? Miłosz Brzeziński? He does podcasts, books, something like that. I sent you the link. Listen to it.

Patryk didn't listen. Instead, he watched two hours of videos about how other people achieve success, and felt even worse.

---

Months passed. The alarm on his phone rang every day at 6:00. Patryk switched it off every day at 6:01. Weeks passed. He broke his own records in avoiding his own life.

Until one morning, instead of scrolling past, he stopped on a reel that happened to appear on his feed. A guy with glasses, in a calm voice, was talking about the feeling of inadequacy.

_"We often think we should be better because others are better. But those others think the same. Everyone thinks that. And that's the trap."_

Patryk stared at the screen. The guy went on:

_"The feeling that you're inadequate is not proof that you're weak. It's proof that you believe things that aren't true. You believe others know better. You believe comparing yourself makes sense. You believe that if you don't achieve something great, you're a nobody."_

Patryk swallowed.

– How does he know that? – he asked the empty room.

_"The truth is that no one knows what they're doing. No one. There's no instruction manual. There's no plan. There are only people who learned to act in spite of not knowing. And that's the whole secret. It's not about knowing. It's about acting."_

---

He finally found him. That Brzeziński. Not in one place, but in several. Podcast episodes, fragments of lectures, interviews. The more he listened, the more something inside him cracked. Not because the man said revolutionary things. On the contrary. He said things that Patryk had always known somewhere deep down. He just had never allowed himself to hear them.

_"Building self-esteem is not about great achievements. It's about small, closed chapters. About the fact that today you did something you didn't do yesterday. Even if it's only... making your bed."_

– Making the bed? – Patryk repeated in disbelief. – That's supposed to be the recipe for life?

And yet the next morning, for the first time in months, he didn't just switch off the alarm. He got up. He went to the bathroom. He brushed his teeth. Then he went back to the bedroom and **made the bed**. Not perfectly, but he **made it**.

And he felt... different. Not better. Different.

He took a pen and wrote on a piece of paper: _"Day 1. I made my bed."_

He didn't know yet that this would be the beginning of something that would change his life.

---

A week later he bought a thick, black notebook. On the first page he wrote: _"Journal of small successes. Not for others. For me."_

And he started writing. Every day. Even when he had nothing to write, he wrote: _"I survived another day. That's also a success."_

At first he felt ridiculous. A man his age, with his education, with his job – keeping a little journal like a teenager. But after a few days he noticed something strange. He was beginning to notice things he hadn't seen before. Small victories. Tiny moments of agency.

_"I made myself coffee without milk, even though I like it with milk."_

_"I went for a walk, even though it was raining."_

_"I called Mum, even though I didn't feel like talking."_

Each sentence was tiny. Each seemed trivial. But together they formed something Patryk couldn't name. Maybe a foundation. Maybe a beginning.

And then he went back to Brzeziński's material. He listened once more to the episode about meaning.

_"Meaning is not something you find. Meaning is something you create. You can make it up. You can invent it. And that's all right. What matters is that it works."_

Patryk thought about his journal. Was this meaning? Or just another exercise in self-deception? Or maybe he didn't have to choose. Maybe he could simply act and see what happens.

---

He started running. Not because he wanted to. Because somewhere he'd heard that if you wait for the desire, you'll never do anything. He put on his shoes, left the house and ran his first kilometre. Then a second. Then a third.

When he got home, panting and soaked with sweat, he stood in front of the mirror and looked himself in the eye. And then he saw something he hadn't seen in years. A smile.

Not because he was proud of himself. Because he had done something he hadn't wanted to do. And survived. And that was more than he had felt in months.

He wrote in his journal: _"Day 23. I ran 5 km. I didn't die."_

---

A month later he signed up for the gym. Six months later for dance classes. A year later he signed up for a marathon.

Not because he thought he would win. Not because he wanted to prove something to others. Because he needed a goal. He needed something that would pull him forward when all other motivation failed.

And then something clicked.

One evening, as he sat over his thick journal, he realised that a year had passed. In all that time he hadn't missed a single day. There were no great revolutions. There were no sudden transformations. There were only small steps that led nowhere – but they led.

He took a pen and wrote:

_"Day 365. I still don't know what I'm doing. I still sometimes feel inadequate. I still have bad days. But I know one thing: I'm here. And that's more than I thought I could manage."_

He closed the notebook and looked out the window. Outside it was raining. The same kind of rain as a year ago, when he first heard those words. Only he was different.

Not better. Not worse. Simply different.

---

The next day he went for a run. It was raining. It was cold. He felt like turning back after the first hundred metres. But he didn't turn back. He ran the whole distance, and then one more kilometre. Not because he had to. Because he could.

And then he understood what it was all about. Not achievements. Not records. Not being better than others. It was about being better than yesterday's self. About acting in spite of fear. About finding strength within yourself, even when you don't feel it.

And if you don't have the strength – make it up.

He came home, took off his wet clothes and sat down at his desk. He opened the notebook to the first page and read:

_"Day 1. I made my bed."_

And then he smiled. Because he knew that what had started with a simple **making of the bed** had led him to a place he never thought he'd reach. To a place where he was finally at peace with himself.

He took a pen and wrote below:

_"Day 366. I made my bed. And I ran. And I'm alive. And that's enough."_

Then he got up, got dressed and left for work. Through the door, down the stairs, across the street. And he felt that every step was his own choice. His own work.

And that was the most important thing.

---

**THE END**
