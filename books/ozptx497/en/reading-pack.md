## Text

**CAT 1011**

## I. THE BEGINNING

Ada woke up at 3:33 in the morning with the terrifying certainty that she was not human.

She lay in the dark, in her flat in Warsaw's Powiśle district, and felt something pulsing inside her palm — deep, steady, as if a second heart were beating just beneath the skin. For a moment she thought it was just a dream, that it would pass, that she would return to normal. But she didn't.

She sat up in bed. Before her eyes, even though the room was dark, images appeared — not colours, not shapes, but something in between. As if someone were projecting onto her retina a film she couldn't control. Pyramids. A desert. Someone with a face she couldn't remember, speaking in a language she understood, though she had never learned it.

— This is not an illness — she whispered to herself. — It's the vibrant health of an ancient being trapped inside a sick body.

She didn't know where the words had come from. She didn't know why she'd said them. But when they were spoken, she felt something inside her crack. As if the concrete wall she'd been building her whole life had begun to crumble.

She got up and walked to the window. Warsaw was asleep, only a few lights burning here and there in the windows. Normal life. Normal people. And she stood there, feeling that she was in the wrong place, at the wrong time, in the wrong body.

— You did this with full premeditation — she heard in her head a voice that wasn't hers. — You chose this path in order to experience this violent splitting apart.

Ada closed her eyes. And for the first time in years she allowed herself to believe that this inner rupture wasn't a sign of madness, but proof of something far older.

---

## II. THE DEVELOPMENT

The next day Ada didn't go to work. She called in sick and switched off her phone. All morning she sat in the kitchen, staring at the steaming tea that had long since gone cold.

She tried to remember when it had begun. Maybe in childhood, when as a five-year-old she told her mother about the "luminous people" who came to her at night. Her mother had put it down to imagination. Back then Ada had thought so too. Until now.

She switched on the computer. In the browser she opened a tab she'd saved a week earlier, after a sleepless night when she felt something calling her. The page was called "Syrian Wisdom — seven levels of consciousness". She scrolled to the first pinned comment, which contained a link to a "digital guide".

She hesitated. It looked like everything she hated — esoterica, pseudoscience, a marketing gimmick. But in her head the same voice as at night spoke up:

_"This is not just a book. It's a tangible navigational map and a compass for those of us who have brutally strayed from the prescribed routes."_

She clicked.

The page loaded with strange speed. On the screen appeared text that seemed to write itself, letter by letter, as if someone were dictating it from another dimension:

_"Stop. Close your eyes now for a brief moment and observe those images that appear on their own, pulsing behind your eyelids without the slightest participation of your will. Feel that deep physical pulsing right in the middle of your palm."_

Ada looked at her hand. It was pulsing. Exactly as at night.

_"The memory-wipers do absolutely everything to make you take this quiet pain of constant inner restlessness for ordinary earthly routine. But the truth inside you resonates. The illusion falls with a crash."_

She read it three times. Then she got up, walked to the mirror and looked at her reflection. A woman past thirty, tired, with bags under her eyes. An ordinary, normal woman.

And yet something in her eyes was different. As if behind her pupils hid a light she couldn't name.

— Who are you? — she asked her reflection.

The reflection didn't answer. But a voice appeared in her head:

_"My name is Sirun, and I come as a messenger to register your unconventional choice."_

Ada closed her eyes. And then she saw what she'd been afraid to see for years: herself in another place, another time, another body. A priestess in the sands, carrying on her shoulders the same terrifying sense of alienation that had woken her today at 3:33 in the morning.

— It wasn't a mistake — she whispered. — I chose this path.

---

## III. THE TURNING POINT

For the next three days Ada didn't leave the house. She read the digital guide from cover to cover, memorising every word, every symbol, every code. She learned about the five Syrian signs that strike the biology of awakened souls: the resonance piercing, the spatial dyscorrelation, the blood-frequency barrier, the awakening of geometry and the rejection of the matrix.

And she recognised herself in them.

Resonance piercing — she felt it every day, that steady pulsing in her palm that appeared for no reason, in the middle of the night, in the middle of the day.

Spatial dyscorrelation — her own flat, where she'd lived for five years, sometimes seemed foreign to her, as if she were seeing it for the first time.

Blood-frequency barrier — she'd tried to tell her mother about it, about those strange feelings, about the voice of Sirun. Her mother had looked at her with a mixture of fear and irritation and said: "Ada, maybe you should see a doctor."

Awakening of geometry — at night, when she closed her eyes, she felt the space around her change shape, recalling ancient pyramidal chambers.

And finally, the rejection of the matrix — that sudden, icy feeling that everything the people around her chased, career, status, security, was utterly empty for her.

On the fourth night, when the clock struck 3:33, Ada heard Sirun's voice for the last time:

_"We are now sealing in your nervous system these awakened codes and establishing around you an unbreakable barrier of pure light. No one and nothing on this very dense and low orbit will ever again be able to lower your recovered frequency."_

Ada felt something inside her close. As if the old code she'd always carried had finally been rewritten into a new one. She got up from the bed, walked to the window and looked at the night sky. Somewhere out there, among the stars, was another version of her. The priestess of the sands. The lone wanderer. The navigator of error. The destroyer of illusions.

All those archetypes were inside her. They always had been.

— I'm coming back — she whispered to the night.

And then, for the first time in weeks, she felt peace. Not that false, imposed peace of daily routine, but a real, deep one that flowed from the very centre of her being.

---

## IV. THE ENDING

A month later Ada returned to work. But she was no longer the same person who had left the office three weeks earlier, pretending everything was fine. She gave up her full-time job. She started her own website, where she wrote about the Syrian codes, about awakening, about lone wanderers. She didn't expect anyone to understand. But she knew she had to do it.

One evening, sitting in her kitchen, she received a message from a stranger. Someone had written: "I've read your texts. For weeks I've been feeling the same pulsing in my palm. I thought I'd gone mad. Thank you for existing."

Ada read the message three times. Then she got up, walked to the window and looked at the sky. Somewhere out there, among the stars, Sirun was still broadcasting his signal. But now she knew she wasn't alone.

— You're not alone — she replied in a message. — And you never were. It's just the matrix that made you think so.

She sent the message and went back to writing. Outside the window Warsaw glittered in the night lights, but she knew that was just an illusion. The real light was elsewhere. Inside.

And at last, for the first time in years, Ada knew where she was going.

---

## V. THE EPILOGUE

A year later Ada stood in the desert in Egypt, in a place she'd dreamed of since childhood. Before her rose a pyramid, and in her palm pulsed the same steady beat that had accompanied her forever.

— You have come — she heard a voice. Not in her ears. Inside.

She turned around. Behind her stood a figure — not quite human, not quite not. Luminous, as if built of stardust and memory.

— Sirun — she said.

— You called me that. But that's not my name. It's a code. Just like yours.

Ada smiled. All her life she'd thought her name was Ada. But now she knew it was only a label pinned to her in this incarnation. Her real name was written in the code she carried in her DNA.

— Why did I come here? — she asked.

— To remember why you chose this path. — The figure reached out a hand. — Are you ready?

Ada took a deep breath. For a moment she thought about her life on Earth — about the mother who never understood her, about the work that was only an illusion, about the loneliness that had been her greatest teacher.

And then she took Sirun's hand.

— I'm coming back — she said.

And then, for the first time in thousands of years, she remembered everything.

---

**THE END**
