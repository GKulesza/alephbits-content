## Text

**FORBIDDEN ENERGY**

When the architect Helena Nowak first saw the plans of the Main Station in Warsaw, she thought it was a mistake. It was 2024, and she was browsing the digital archives, looking for inspiration for a new interchange centre. She came across a document that should not exist – an original technical design from 1889, with annotations that made no sense.

– What is this? – she asked her assistant, who looked over her shoulder.

– It looks like a wiring diagram.

– But that's impossible. The station had gas lighting until 1915.

The assistant shrugged. Helena enlarged the image. In the margin someone had written by hand: "Resonance system No. 4 – tested successfully, June 1888." Below it were symbols that looked like circuit diagrams, but resembled nothing she knew. There were spirals, parallel lines and markings that did not match any electrical system she had ever seen.

Over the following weeks, Helena immersed herself in the archives. She found similar annotations in the station designs of Kraków, Poznań, Wrocław. Everywhere the same pattern – systems that should not exist, markings nobody could explain, and dates that predated the official introduction of electricity by decades.

Then she started looking beyond Poland. Grand Central in New York, Leipzig Hauptbahnhof in Germany, Victoria Terminus in Mumbai – all of them held similar secrets. Great halls of marble and copper, heating systems that seemed too efficient for the age of coal, and towers that, by her calculations, could have served as antennas.

– It's impossible – she kept telling herself, but the evidence kept piling up.


**The Meeting**

A month later, Helena went to Berlin for an architectural conference. During a break, an older woman with grey hair and piercing eyes came up to her.

– Mrs. Helena Nowak? – she asked in Polish, although her accent sounded foreign.

– Yes, that's me.

– My name is Dr. Erika Schmidt. I work at the Institute for the History of Technology in Munich. I've seen your publications about the Warsaw stations. We need to talk.

They sat down in a corner of the conference hall. Erika took an old photograph out of her folder – a building Helena did not recognize.

– This is the station in Breslau – said Erika. – Demolished in 1945. But before the war there was a system there that scientists called "free energy". Nobody knows how it worked. The plans disappeared in 1944.

– What does "free energy" mean?

– It means these buildings were not powered by coal or steam. They were powered by something else. Something that stopped working in the 1920s. Or something that was switched off.

Helena felt her heart begin to beat faster.

– Who switched it off?

– That is the question I have been looking for the answer to for thirty years.


**The Secret**

Over the following months, Helena and Erika worked together. They analysed hundreds of documents, photographs and architectural plans. They discovered that between 1880 and 1920, over a thousand similar stations had been built all over the world. All of them had identical features – tall halls, marble walls, copper roofs, complicated ventilation systems, and mysterious technical rooms that the documentation described as "storage rooms" or "ventilation ducts".

But the real breakthrough was the discovery that these buildings were connected to each other – not only by rails, but by something more. Helena found old railway maps on which not only the train routes were marked, but also transmission lines. At first glance they looked like ordinary tracks, but on closer inspection they turned out to be systems of conductors that could transmit energy over enormous distances.

– It was a network – Helena said one evening, as they sat in her Warsaw apartment. – A global energy network. The stations were the nodes. And the tracks were the conductors.

– And something destroyed it – added Erika. – Or someone.

– Who?

– Maybe those who didn't want people to have access to free energy.

Helena stood up and walked to the window. Beyond the pane stretched Warsaw – a city that had survived wars, destruction and rebuilding. Somewhere beneath her feet, in the cellars of the Central Station, systems still lay that nobody understood.

– What if it's still working? – she asked.

– What if?


**The Discovery**

A few weeks later, Helena found herself in the cellars of the Central Station. She had permission to study the foundations as part of a planned modernisation. She descended to a level nobody knew about – to vaults that did not appear on any plans.

There were pipes, cables and devices that looked as if they were from another era. Helena touched one of them – it was cold, but when she held a special meter against it, the needle twitched.

– It's impossible – she whispered.

The meter showed the presence of energy. Weak, barely detectable, but there.

She ran to her office and called Erika.

– I've found something. The system is still working.

– How is that possible? – asked Erika.

– I don't know. Maybe nobody ever switched it off. Maybe it was waiting.


**The End**

Helena returned to the cellars the next day with a group of engineers. For weeks they analysed the system, trying to understand how it worked. They discovered that the station had been built on the principle of resonance – it used the Earth's natural frequencies to generate energy. That explained why it needed no coal or steam. It also explained why the buildings had such high ceilings and copper roofs – they were part of the receiving system.

But what they found at the end was the most surprising of all. The system was not Polish, nor German, nor American. It was ancient. Beneath the station's foundations, an older structure had been discovered – stone blocks with carved symbols that matched those from the 1889 plans.

– This wasn't built in the nineteenth century – Helena said to Erika over the phone. – It was unearthed. They only restored what already existed.

– So what was it? – asked Erika.

– I don't know. But I think it was something that existed long before us. And something that is still waiting for us to rediscover it.

Helena put down the phone. She looked up at the station ceiling. The lights flickered, trains departed and arrived, people hurried about their business. Nobody knew they were standing on top of something that could change everything.

And maybe that was exactly why the secret still endured.


**THE END**
